# Design

## Context

The demo pass (2026-10-20; notes, transcripts and videos kept privately) ran nine tasks through `mobile-agent-mcp` with Claude Code 2.1.304 on `claude-opus-5-5`, one fresh `claude -p` per task: iOS 116 turns, 104 server calls, 9 failed; Android 58, 50, 0. The host: macOS 27.0.1, Xcode 27.1 (27A9275), the iOS 26.5 runtime (23F77), the only one installed; the README's quick start, which lists Settings' General row as `@e11`, ran under Xcode 27.0 (27A266a). The acceptance of `mcp-server` (`reports/12.1-mcp-acceptance.md`) ran the fixture app on the same host with no visibility problem: the fixture's screens are SwiftUI lists and forms; Settings' root is a UIKit collection view, Calendar's sheet and Maps' search field live in sheets over the map. The listing's visibility comes from `crates/mirror`'s visibility module: what a tap reaches (`reach`, the 8-point floor), what a view shows through its scroll containers' clipping (`Sight::shows`), the covers (bars, keyboards, dialogs, pinned headers), as the archived `rn-app-fixes` change built them (its design D31 to D57) against React Native screens.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- Settings', Calendar's and Maps' on-screen elements listed and reachable as the fixture's are, with a fake screen of the same geometry holding the fix.
- Replies that spare a model a snapshot or a screenshot: `type`'s read-back, usable refs in a wait's summary, a switch's value, the found element, the alert in front, the screen's size in points.
- The server's bound a cap, never a default above the daemon's.
- A second pass on the same tasks with fewer turns and no failed call from the tool's side, counted.

**Non-Goals:**
- Safari's web content in the iOS tree (deferred in `docs/overview.md`; Android's Chrome lists its webview).
- A batch tool or parallel calls in the server.
- The Android alarm row's name (" Alarm" without the time), which is the app's accessibility label.

## Decisions

### D1. Spike S14 before the mirror's fix
The visibility fault is reproduced with the CLI on the Simulator's Settings root, Calendar's New Event sheet and Maps' search field: the full and interactive listings, `get box` of the missing elements, the windows and scroll containers the replica holds with their frames and content sizes, and the visibility module's inputs for one missing row (which container clips it, which cover, which floor), against the fixture's List tab on the same host, which lists. The spike names the cause: a container whose frame or content size the clipping misreads under this runtime, a window the replica misplaces, a cover that is not one, or something else; and whether Xcode 27.1 or the iOS 26.5 runtime changed what the accessibility bridge reports (the fixture's and the quick start's listings under 27.0 are the comparison; an older runtime is not downloaded for this). It also measures the switch: a plain tap and holds of 40, 80 and 150 ms on Settings' Bold Text switch and on the fixture's Animate toggle, ten each, to see which flips a UIKit switch. Report `docs/spikes/s14-settings-visibility.md`; go when the cause is in the mirror's computation (task 2 fixes it), no-go when it is in what the bridge reports (then task 2 gates a reading on the case, as `rn-app-fixes` gated the RN cadence). S14 (2026-10-21, `docs/spikes/s14-settings-visibility.md`): go; every geometric input is right and the three elements are hidden by one rule, `cover_by_later_siblings` (D31 of `rn-app-fixes`), which takes a later sibling that shows something as drawn over its whole frame, where `_UIFloatingBarContainerView` spans the window (0,0 402x874) and draws only its toolbar (0,788 402x86) in Settings and Calendar, and Maps' list "Locations" has the sheet's frame with a 73-point inset under the search bar; the bridge reports whole frames as before, on the same runtime build, and the quick start's listing predates the rule.

### D2. The fix is held by geometry, not by the app
Task 2 adds the failing case to the fake driver's screens or the mirror's fixtures with the geometry the spike recorded (the window, the container's frame and content size, the row's bounds), writes the listing test red, then changes the computation; the verification on the real apps is task 5's. No per-app rule, no "Settings" in the code: lean core. As built (task 2.1, 2026-10-21): a later sibling whose frame holds the earlier sibling whole, and that shows nothing itself and is no bar, is a host: it covers what shows in it, each bar by its frame, each scroll container by its cells and each node that shows something itself by its bounds, not its frame. A later sibling that overlaps the earlier one in part is a bar or a float: it covers its frame, as the archived cases need. The two shapes are the mirror's fixtures `floating-toolbar-ios`, the floating bar's container spanning the window after the transition view and drawing only its toolbar, 0,788 402x86, and `search-sheet-ios`, the sheet's list view of the content view's frame after the search bar's view, its list's first cell 73 points lower, with the map's buttons above the sheet; their listing tests were red on the frame rule, no row listed in the first, the search field and the map's buttons counted under "the list" and "the overlay" in the second, and are green. The frame rule keeps five archived shapes: the consent screen's footer, 740 to 874 points over a scroll view from 118; the feed's header listed after its list, 0 to 118 over a list running to 874; the creation tab's footer, 764 to 874 over a list from 122, whose band above its tab bar covers the controls' strip; the two controls behind a header listed after its list, reaching above the screen; and the edit menu's window as the reader bounds it, 121 to 165 over the application's window across the screen. Each lies over part of the view before it, laid out there to be drawn over that part, while the floating bar's container and the sheet's list view hold the whole view before them in frames their parents' size and draw only where what they hold draws: the containment condition tells the two apart where nothing else in the tree does. Without it the rule failed 12 of the workspace's tests on those five shapes; with it one test's counts changed, `a_hint_never_calls_a_window_over_the_screen_a_header`, whose window across the screen is a shape the reader stopped giving at rn-app-fixes 7.1, the test keeping its point, no header or footer named. `just micro --filter daemon/` at a 1-minute load of 3.81 (5.71 and 6.45 over 5 and 15 minutes, other sessions' benchmarks running): the socket's interactive snapshot of the fake Big tab 823.7 µs against the baseline's 773.8, +6.4% and over the 5% threshold, the request's parse 0.323 µs against 0.365 with 4 allocations against 8, the reply line 95.4 µs against 93.1, the same; the baseline is 320 commits older, of 2026-10-11 on macOS 26.6.2 against this host's 27.0.1, and that snapshot is a fake Android emulator's, whose tree the host rule never runs on: the same benchmark with the daemon of the commit before the rule and with the rule's, three alternating rounds each at a load of 5.2 to 5.8, gave 994.7 µs before and 980.2 µs after (the median of the rounds' medians), -1.5%, the same.

### D3. `type` reads back, and does not retry
`type` enters text into the focused field with no target; after the entry, the daemon reads the focused element's value as `fill`'s read-back does (`read_back` in `handlers/act.rs`, the same hold and word diff), and the reply carries `text`, `verified`, `readFrom` and `method`, the CLI's line `✓ Done (read back "example.com", typed)` as `fill`'s. A mismatch is a warning naming what the field holds, not a retry: `type` appends, so entering again would double the text; the warning says to `fill` the field instead. No focused field in the tree: `not read back` with a warning saying so. As built (task 3.1, 2026-10-21): the read-back reads the field the keys went into, or without a target the text or search field the full listing marks focused, through `fill`'s `read_back` and its 200 ms hold after keys; it takes the text as shown when the field holds what it held followed by the text, the text alone (a selection or a placeholder replaced), as many mask characters as it held and the text has, or the words the entry changed spelling the text, D43's word diff of `rn-app-fixes` (`compare_typed`). The reply keeps `typed`, the request's text, and gains `text`, what the field holds as the read-back read it, so that the line names "exampl" when it differs from the text; `verified` is `text` or `length`, `false` with `reason` `text_mismatch` or `no_value_to_read`, null with `no_focused_field`; and `readFrom` and `readBackMs` as `fill`'s. The line is `✓ Done (read back "exampl", typed)` beside the warning, which names what the field holds and advises `fill`, and `✓ Done (not read back, typed)` without a focused field; an empty text is not read back. The fake's existing `--fake-typing max:6` drops the keys past the sixth, as the address bar did, so no fake mode was added; the code step whose input lies outside the tree holds the case with no focused field.

### D4. A wait's summary issues its refs
The screen summary a `wait_timeout` carries is a listing; the handler that builds it registers its refs with the session as a snapshot does (`remember`), so a model may act on what it saw. Alternative, a summary without refs (rejected: the summary is there to be acted on). As built (task 3.2, 2026-10-21): the mirror's timeout carries its summary without the refs' issue to a session, so the daemon writes the summary of a `wait_timeout` from an interactive snapshot of its own, which it keeps as the session's last snapshot with `remember`, its refs the session's and its listing the baseline of `diff snapshot` and `wait --change`; the summary an `element_not_found` prints is the same listing and issues its refs the same way.

### D5. Replies name what the screen holds
`get value` on a switch returns the accessibility value the tree carries (`on` or `off` as the platform's value reads, or the raw value), and `no value` as a line when the element has none; `wait --text` answers with the element found, `✓ Found text "Welcome, agent" @e12`, as `wait <selector>` names its element; `open` returns as soon as a system alert window is in front of the app, `✓ Opened <app>; an alert "..." is in front` with a warning naming the alert, since the next action is the model's (the dialog covers are the mirror's already); the screenshot reply carries `points` (the screen's size in points) beside the image's pixels and the scale, the CLI's line and the server's text say `460x1000 of a 402x874-point screen`, and `invalid_argument` for coordinates off the screen says the screen's size in points and that a screenshot's pixels are not points. As built (task 3.2, 2026-10-21): `get value` carries `checked`, the listing's state of a switch or a checkbox, beside `value`, the line `on`, `off`, the value, or `no value` for neither. A wait for a text or an element names what it found, `✓ Found button "Sign in" @e4`, the ref the full listing's for an element that bears one when no snapshot issued it yet; a plain text bears none, so the fixture's status reads `✓ Found text "Welcome, agent"`. `Mirror::system_dialog` names the top-most modal that cuts the screen when a window system UI owns holds it, `alert "Allow …"` from SpringBoard's tree on iOS and `dialog "Permission"` for the permission controller's window on Android, as a refused action names it; `open` returns at it, unsettled, its reply's `alert` and its warning naming it, and names one that came in front while the screen settled; the line stays `✓ Opened <app> on <target>`, the warning, `alert "…" stands in front of <app>; answer it before you act on the app: a snapshot lists its buttons`, carrying the alert. The fake's location prompt stays in front of its fixture while the fixture runs, so a second `open` meets it, and no launch option was added. The screenshot reply carries `points {width, height}` from the driver's screen; the CLI's line and the server's text, beside the image or on the line after the path, are `460x1000 of a 402x874-point screen`, which puts part of task 4.1's screenshot text in place; a click on `(460, 1000)` there fails `invalid_argument`: "(460, 1000) lies off the screen, 402x874 points: click takes points, not a screenshot's pixels, which are scaled; a screenshot's reply gives its pixels beside the screen's points". As built (task 5.4, 2026-10-21): `get value` and `get text` on an element that holds a switch sharing its identifier, or that such a switch holds, read that switch, the one D6's clause presses, through the same resolution (`aim::switch_of`) on every target, while `get box` gives the element's own bounds: on the second pass on real apps `get value #ENHANCE_TEXT_LEGIBILITY` read the table cell around Bold Text's switch, `no value` with the switch on, `get text` there read the cell's empty name, and both reads of On/Off Labels' named cell its value, the switch's state as a raw 0. The fake's switches board held the case, red on the cell's null value without a state, then green; on this session's own Simulator `get value #ENHANCE_TEXT_LEGIBILITY` read off, on, off and on as three clicks by the identifier flipped Bold Text, the full listing's state agreeing each time, and `get text` read "Bold Text"; `#ON_OFF_LABELS` read the same way with "On/Off Labels", and the label's ref read the switch's state.

### D6. The switch press, gated
If S14 finds a plain tap does not flip a UIKit switch while a hold does, the Simulator driver's tap on an element whose role is a switch holds for the shortest duration the spike found reliable, gated on that role and platform; the fixture's SwiftUI toggle and Android's switches, which flip on a tap, keep the tap. If S14 finds the tap lands off the switch's thumb or the row takes it, the fix is where the finding points. S14 (2026-10-21): a plain tap, its up within 0.1 ms of its down, flipped Settings' Bold Text 0 of 10 and the fixture's Animate toggle 0 of 10 (1 of 10 on its labelled element), while holds of 40, 80 and 150 ms flipped both 10 of 10 each: the press on a switch holds 40 ms, the shortest measured, gated on the switch role on the Simulator and taking in SwiftUI's toggle, a UISwitch underneath; and it aims at the control inside a labelled switch, since Settings' labelled one, 330 points wide with its centre on the label, flipped 0 of 40 at any hold while the 63-point control inside it flipped 30 of 30 with a hold. As built (task 3.3, 2026-10-21): `click` on a target of the Simulator class holds the press 40 ms (`SWITCH_HOLD`) on an element of the switch role, unless the request names its own hold, and aims it at the first switch the full listing lists below that element, a labelled switch's control, by its ref (`control_within`); a switch with none below it takes the press itself, and a click on a point, Android's targets and physical iPhones keep the plain tap. The fake's iOS switch holds an unnamed 63x28 control at its right end, as the reader gives UIKit's switch cells and SwiftUI's toggles. On this session's own Simulator Settings' Bold Text flipped 10 of 10 by the switch's ref and 10 of 10 by `text=Bold Text`, each press at the control's centre, 336.5,160.33, its dispatch 40.6 to 50.3 ms, and the fixture's Animate 10 of 10 by `#anim-toggle`, at 239.5,284. `#ENHANCE_TEXT_LEGIBILITY` named the table cell around the labelled switch, which shares the identifier with the switch and with the switch's label, a 71-point button, and comes first in the tree, so the click was a plain tap at the cell's centre, on the label, which flipped 0 of 10, as ten plain taps on the label itself did; On/Off Labels' named cell shares its identifier with its label, a text, and its control, a switch. Selectors resolve as they did, an innermost match landing on the label in both rows, and the gate takes one clause more: an element that holds a switch sharing its identifier, or that such a switch holds, presses that switch's control as a click on the switch does, whichever selector or ref named it (`switch_control`), the fake's switches board holding both shapes; Bold Text by `#ENHANCE_TEXT_LEGIBILITY` then flipped 10 of 10 and On/Off Labels by `#ON_OFF_LABELS` 10 of 10, each press at the control's centre, 336.5,160.33 and 330.5,318.67, its dispatch 40.2 to 45.2 ms.

### D7. The server's bound caps
The server passes `--call-timeout` less the margin as a command's `timeout` only when the call gives a larger one; a call that gives none keeps the daemon's default (its session's, else 10 s), so a `click` on a missing element fails at 10 s with `element_not_found` and the model asks for longer with `timeout` where it means to wait. `wait` keeps the same rule: the card and the tool's description say to pass `timeout` for a wait past 10 s. This reverses `mcp-server`'s D7 insertion, whose aim, the daemon's wait ending before the server's bound, the cap meets. The screenshot result's text states the sizes (D5). The card says that a click which reports no change may precede the app's reaction, and that `wait` with `change` collects it, which the Photos task showed (a push 428 ms after the tap reported none). As built (task 4.1, 2026-10-21): `Tool::call` in `crates/mcp/src/tools.rs` keeps the cap alone, a `timeout` or `ms` above the bound less the 2 s margin capped to it and said on the warning line, and gives no timeout to a call that names none, so the daemon's default rules, the session's, else 10 s, 3 s for a settle, and for an `open` 10 s for the first tree then 3 s to settle; the backstop stays. `wait`'s description ends "pass `timeout` for a wait past the default of 10 s"; the card's waiting paragraph says that a click which reports no change may precede the app's reaction, which `wait` with `change: true` then collects, and that waits for a change, a text or an element and a selector's search end at 10 s unless the call passes `timeout`, and its tools paragraph names the cap, "a timeout above 53 s is capped to 53 s" under the default bound: 2,865 characters in all and 2,983 read-only, the first paragraph's 369 unchanged, `help`'s table 32 lines with its `--timeout` row naming the default and the cap. The screenshot's text is task 3.2's, held by the server's `screenshots` tests. Through the server on the fake Android target with `--call-timeout 30`, a click on a selector that matches nothing failed with `element_not_found` and a wait for a text that never appears ended with `wait_timeout`, each at 28.0 s on the bound's timeout and at 10.0 s on the daemon's default; `wait`'s description, the card's sentences and the unit test of a call's line were red, then green, and the cap is held by the server's tests of a timeout above the bound, the wait past the bound among them.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [The cause is in the bridge, not the mirror] → D1's no-go path gates a reading on the case, measured; the fake screen still holds the behavior.
- [`type`'s read-back on a field that is not focused reads the wrong element] → read only the element the tree marks focused; otherwise `not read back` and the warning.
- [A 10 s default makes a slow app's waits fail where 53 s passed] → the card and descriptions say to pass `timeout`; the acceptance's tasks passed with the daemon's defaults before `mcp-server` raised them.
