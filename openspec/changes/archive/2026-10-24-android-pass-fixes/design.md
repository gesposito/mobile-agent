# Design

## Context

The third pass's Android failures (notes kept privately): `open com.android.chrome` answered `✗ adb_failed: shell,v2,raw:am start -W … Resource temporarily unavailable (os error 35)`; the next `snapshot` answered `deadline_exceeded` at 10 s; a full snapshot and a screenshot then took 11 and 12 s. The emulator (`pixel_api36`, 2 GB of RAM in its configuration, 4 cores) had 2.39 of 2.53 GB in use and 697 MB of swap. Through the CLI, Settings' cold `open` took 14.65 s with `firstTreeMs` 14,438 while `am start -W` alone reported 6.3 to 6.9 s; warm, 0.72 s. The driver's shell path to adb is blocking, each read and write under the client's 10 s timeout (D1). `Mirror::hit` returns the element a tap reaches at a point through `visibility::landing`, and the click reports it when the session holds its ref. A stale ref's twin (`Mirror::twin`) took 2.38 and 2.62 s end to end in the pass's two cases.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- No launch fails on a timeout shorter than the command's own.
- Every click names what it hit.
- A twin costs what a lookup costs.
- The first-tree wait on Android understood, with numbers, and cut where it is the daemon's.

**Non-Goals:**
- The Material time picker's unref'd overlay, `wait --text` matching a suggestion, the click's 200 ms window on Android (recorded as limitations).
- Changing the user's AVD; the emulator's memory is set per run with its `-memory` flag.

## Decisions

### D1. A launch takes the command's deadline
The Android driver's shell transport is blocking: each connection to the adb server reads and writes under the client's 10 s timeout (`SO_RCVTIMEO`, `SO_SNDTIMEO`), and nothing in the shell path is non-blocking; the fake agent and the daemon's server, which read again on `WouldBlock`, poll a listener set non-blocking or read under a 5 ms timeout, and retry no error. macOS reports an expired read timeout as `EAGAIN`, "Resource temporarily unavailable (os error 35)", which Rust gives as `ErrorKind::WouldBlock`: a 15-line program reading a silent loopback socket under a 300 ms read timeout got that kind and that code after 301 ms. So the pass's `adb_failed` on Chrome's `open` was `am start -W`, which prints its `Starting:` line and then nothing until the app's first frame, outlasting the 10 s read timeout on the starved emulator, where Settings' `am start -W` alone took 6.3 to 6.9 s; a read again on `WouldBlock` bounded by that timeout would end where the timeout ends and change nothing. The fix is the deadline, not a retry: `AppControl::launch` takes the command's own deadline, `open`'s start plus its timeout, the waiting commands' default (10 s) when it names none, and the Android driver reads `am start -W`'s output with the time left to that deadline instead of the fixed 10 s, so a slow cold start completes within what `open` was given and a longer `timeout` gives it more; a launch that outlasts the deadline fails with `deadline_exceeded`, naming the launch and the deadline, never with the socket's error. The second launch that brings a running app to the front (D4 of action-verdicts) takes the explicit timeout's deadline, else as long again from its own start. The other shell commands keep the fixed 10 s, none having that shape: none waits on the device for longer than it bounds itself, the agent's start already reads under its own deadline, and the stop path's poll for the agent's exit, silent until it ends, is bounded by its own 2 or 3 s. Measured by tests on the adb client and the driver against a fake adb server whose answer to `am start -W` comes after the client's read timeout.

As built (task 1.1, 2026-10-24): `AppControl::launch(app, launch, deadline)` takes the launching command's deadline on every driver, the Simulator's, the iPhone's and the fakes' leaving it unused; `open` passes its start plus its wait, the explicit timeout or the session's default, and its second launch the explicit timeout's deadline, else that wait again from its own start. The adb client's `Device::shell_until(command, deadline)` reads the output with the time left to the deadline, set before each packet, runs nothing for a deadline already past, and returns at a read that waited out all the time left, perhaps within a packet, a `TimedOut` error saying `the command had not ended by its deadline, <ms> ms after it began`; opening the stream keeps the 10 s. The Android driver's launch, `devices::android::launch_on`, reads `am start -W` so and gives a timeout at the deadline as `deadline_exceeded`, `am start -W had not finished launching <app> by open's deadline, <ms> ms after the launch began; the app may still be starting: open it again with a longer timeout, or take a snapshot to see what is in front`, any other adb failure staying `adb_failed`. Red first with stubs that took the deadline and did not use it: the driver's launch against a fake adb server whose read deadline is 200 ms, `am start -W` silent 600 ms after its `Starting:` line, failed with the pass's own `adb_failed: shell,v2,raw:am start -W … Resource temporarily unavailable (os error 35)` after 204 ms, as did the adb client's test, and `open` given 30 s passed a deadline 10 s after its start; then green: the launch opens within a 3 s deadline, and one silent past a 700 ms deadline fails at it with `deadline_exceeded`; the second launch's deadline was watched red against one taken from its own start always. The workspace's 1,789 tests green with clippy and fmt clean. No emulator ran; the replay of Chrome's open on one is task 3.1's. With no `timeout`, `open`'s deadline is the default 10 s from its start, so a launch slower than that still fails, now as a timeout that says to give `open` a longer one.

### D2. A hit is named without a ref
`Mirror::hit` returns the element a tap reaches at the point whether or not the session holds a ref for it: `hit` carries `role`, `name` and `ref` null, the line `hit text "Address" (no ref)`, so a tap by coordinates or on a plain text says what it landed on; the mismatch warning compares elements, not refs. A keyboard hit stays as it is.

As built (task 1.2, 2026-10-23): `visibility::landing` keeps its pass over the keyboard and the elements with a ref, and where no element with a ref lies under the point in any window takes the top-most element the full listing lists without one (`render::listed_in_full`: a named text or image, a named or identified container, a node of another role), visible, folded into nothing and reached at the point, chosen among the windows and within one as those with a ref are, so every hit with a ref and the keyboard's are as they were; `Mirror::hit` gives it with no ref, none having been issued. The reply's `hit` keeps its shape, `{role, name, ref}`; the CLI's line, which the server's results are, says `(no ref)` after any hit but the keyboard whose `ref` is null, so also after a button a click by selector reached before any snapshot of the session listed it, as two of the server's tests show, whose expectations say so; the warning compares the elements with `holds`, as before. Red on the fake's login screen, a click at (200, 316) on its status answering `"hit":null`, and on the line without `(no ref)`; green with the workspace's 1,783 tests. On this session's Simulator Safari's full listing gave its address bar's title as `text "Address" [id=TabBarItemTitle]: example.com`, without a ref, the full `snapshot --json` carrying no bounds for it, so its point came from `get box` (149, 806, 104x20.33): `click 201 816` answered `"hit":{"name":"Address","ref":null,"role":"text"}` and `✓ Done (hit text "Address" (no ref), dispatch 0.0 ms, first frame change 121.3 ms, first tree delta 156.3 ms)`, the field taking the focus, and a click by coordinates on the Close button beside the field named it by its ref, `"hit":{"name":"Close","ref":"@e39","role":"button"}`.

### D3. A twin costs a lookup
The cost of a twin is measured in the daemon's spans (the stale check, the lookup, any wait between); the lookup itself walks the replica once. Whatever waits before it that the correctness of the action does not need (a settle, a read-back hold that belongs after the entry, a re-read) goes; the measurement after shows a resolved call within the span of a plain one plus the lookup. The numbers go in this decision's "As built".

As built (task 2.1, 2026-10-24): measured first, the twin costs its lookup and nothing waits before it, so nothing was cut. An action on a stale ref goes through `aim::current`: the session's check, `Mirror::resolve`, which applies what arrived and performs the reads already due as it does for any ref, then `Mirror::twin`, which builds the replica's view once and reads nothing; `fill` follows its field to a twin the same way once its focus tap rebuilt it. On the fake's rebuilt board, on both platforms, for both fields, four rounds each: a click on a stale ref took 201 to 207 ms against 201 to 206 ms on the twin's ref, a fill 0.6 to 2.8 ms against 0.9 to 3.7 ms. On this session's Simulator, on a quiet host, with a build that timed the daemon's steps: ten fills of Maps' search box (`MapsSearchTextField`) with "Lisbon" by the ref of the snapshot after `open --relaunch`, which the fill's own focus tap left stale, its twin taken after the tap, took 1,638 to 2,451 ms end to end (median 1,901); ten by the ref a snapshot gave before the relaunch, stale from the start, 1,602 to 2,533 ms (median 1,740), of which the stale check took 0.0 ms and the lookup 0.1 to 0.2 ms; six clicks on the box took 935 to 1,011 ms by a current ref and six 815 to 1,195 ms by a stale one. The wait is each action's own, on a stale ref or not: after the tap that focused the box, the read of Maps' window while it raised its card, 790 to 991 ms, which the fill's wait for the keyboard on iOS (design D34 of rn-app-fixes) needs, 938 to 1,224 ms in all; the keys, 437 to 786 ms; and the read-back of typed text, 201 to 759 ms, a read of the three windows, 177 to 310 ms, and, when frames changed after it began, a read begun after the 200 ms hold (`READ_BACK_HOLD`), 167 to 299 ms, which an app that sets its field's value again needs; a click's watch, about 950 ms, is the read after its tap. So the third pass's 2.38 and 2.62 s were those calls' own costs, not the twin's, and all three waits stay, correctness needing them. Before and after are the same numbers, the path being unchanged. The test is kept as a guard: with nothing waiting before the lookup there was nothing to fix, so it could not be red first. `a_call_on_a_stale_ref_takes_what_the_same_call_on_its_twin_takes` runs on the rebuilt board with each read of the fake's tree taking 80 ms (`FakeDriver::with_read_cost`), so that a read before the lookup shows: a click and a fill on a stale ref take at most 50 ms longer than the same call on the twin's ref, the quickest of three compared, on both platforms, for both fields. Green on the code as it is, the click 201 to 207 ms either way and the fill 163 to 184 ms on the twin's ref against 178 to 184 ms on a stale one on iOS, 81 to 86 against 83 to 90 ms on Android, it was watched red against a mutant that read the tree again before the lookup, the click on the stale ref taking 290.9 ms against 202.9 ms and the fill 261.8 against 170.0 ms, which a fake whose reads cost nothing let pass. The workspace's 1,790 tests green with clippy and fmt clean.

### D4. The first tree on Android, measured
On a cold-booted emulator, quiet host, twice: at the AVD's 2 GB and with `-memory 4096`: `open` of Settings, Clock and Chrome cold and warm through the CLI with `firstTreeMs`, against `am start -W`'s TotalTime for the same launches, and the daemon's attach time. If the daemon's share beyond the app's own launch is seconds on the healthy emulator, the wait is found (the agent's first read, the attach, the poll) and cut; if it is the starved emulator's, the README's Android section says so with the `-memory` flag, and the harness's provenance records the emulator's memory where it records the AVD.

As built (task 2.2, 2026-10-24): the slow opens were the emulator's, and not its memory's: the first tree on Android is the launch's and the agent's read, not a wait of the daemon's, at either memory, and the third pass's 10 to 19 s opens did not come back on a cold-booted emulator on a quiet host at the AVD's 2 GB nor with `-memory 4096`. So nothing in `open` was cut, the README gives the numbers without advice on the memory, which they do not support, and the harness's provenance records the emulator's memory beside its AVD, so that the next slow run shows it. ## Risks / Trade-offs

- [A long deadline hides a wedged adb] → only the launch takes it, bounded by the command's timeout; the timeout names the launch and the deadline.
- [A hit without a ref names a node the model cannot act on] → the line says `(no ref)`; the next listing gives it one if it is actionable.
- [The twin's cost is a wait the correctness needs] → the measurement says which; the design records it rather than cutting it.
