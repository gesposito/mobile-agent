# Design

## Context

The demo passes (notes, transcripts and videos kept privately) ran Claude Code against the server on real apps. The replies that verify, the project's reason to exist, left four cases unverified, all of the same shape: the daemon knew more than it said. At a click's dispatch the replica holds the element under the point, and the click's watch (the 200 ms window that reports the first frame change and tree delta) sees the change but not its subject; a settle (`crates/mirror`'s settle predicate, a quiet window of 200 ms bounded by 3 s) resolves at once when the quiet began before the input's effect; a stale ref (the element gone from the replica) fails although the replica holds the same control again, rebuilt by the app, as task 5.5 of `real-app-fixes` found when `fill`'s read-back learned to find the field again; `open` waits for the app's screen to show and settle but does not check that the app's window is the one in front when the app was already running. The delta object the CLI prints with `--delta` is agent-browser's, byte for byte in `--json` (`crates/cli/README.md`), and the human output prints the same object pretty for a kind other than `full` and `unchanged`.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- Every reply of the four cases says what happened: the element hit, the effect waited for, the twin resolved, the window in front.
- The human output of a delta reads like the listings it comes from.
- No new always-on cost on the click's dispatch path beyond a lookup in the replica the daemon holds.

**Non-Goals:**
- Hit-testing against the app itself (the replica is the model; what the app does with a tap is what the next listing shows).
- A batch tool or parallel calls in the server (one call per turn stays).
- The Android alarm row's name (the app's accessibility label).

## Decisions

### D1. The click names what it hit
At dispatch, the daemon takes from the replica the element under the tap point, the top-most that a tap reaches (the covers and the reach computation of `crates/mirror` as the listing uses them), and the reply carries `hit` (role, name, ref when the session holds one) beside `clicked`; the line reads `✓ Done (hit button "Add alarm", dispatch 0.2 ms, first tree delta 351 ms)`. When the hit element is not the target the call named (the layout moved between the snapshot and the tap, or a view came over it), the reply warns naming both, and the tap still goes in: the daemon checked the target's reach before dispatch, so a mismatch is the replica's change in between, which the warning makes visible. A click by coordinates names what it hit the same way. Alternative, refusing the tap on a mismatch (rejected: the replica may lag the app by a frame; the tap is what the model asked for, the warning is what it needs). As built (task 2.1, 2026-10-22): `Mirror::hit` takes, reading nothing from the target, the top-most element with a ref that a tap reaches at the point (`Sight::reaches`: inside its bounds within its visible bounds before the views drawn over part of it, under none of them, no view covering it whole), the last of the top-most window that holds one, so the innermost or the one drawn over the others, or the keyboard where it draws; the click takes it right after the mirror's `act` returns, from the replica the mirror aimed the tap in, outside `dispatchMs` and before the watch drains what the tap changed. `hit` is `{role, name, ref}`, the ref only when the session's snapshots listed the element, and null when no element with a ref lies under the point; the line names the role and the name, `hit button "Sign in"`, the ref left to the JSON and the spans keeping their one decimal. The warning comes when the element hit is neither the target, nor part of it, nor what holds it (`Mirror::holds`), so a text a selector found is hit through the cell around it without one. A ref is aimed at what a tap reaches of it in that same replica, so its tap lands elsewhere only under a view the covers do not see, which the fake's `banner` board gives: a window of system UI's over a button, inset 8 points from the screen's sides so that it cuts nothing of what a tap reaches of the button, where a click on the button's ref taps its centre, the fake's own hit test lands on the banner, and the reply names `button "placeholder-button-5"` and warns naming both, on both platforms. `--fake-latency` and the rebuilt board give no mismatch: a replica that lags the app is the replica's view, and a rebuilt node a stale ref, task 2.2's. `just micro --filter daemon/` does not time the click path, its suite being a socket snapshot and the wire's parse and reply, and the lookup runs after the dispatch span is taken.

### D2. A settle after an input waits for the effect
`wait --settle` called within 1 s of the session's last input (a click, a fill, a type, a press, a swipe or a scroll; the session records the time) first waits for the first change of the tree or the frames since that input, up to 1 s, then counts the quiet window as today; the reply says `Settled after 612 ms: tree changed last` as today, or `nothing changed within 1 s of the press` when no effect came, which is the fact a model needs. A settle with no input in the last second behaves as today. `open`'s own settle, which follows its launch, waits the same way for the app's first screen. The mirror's settle predicate does not change (ui-mirror's requirement stands); the daemon composes the first-change wait (the change wait of `real-app-fixes`, D8 of `mcp-server`) with the settle. Alternative, a longer quiet window (rejected: it slows every settle for the few that start too early). As built (task 2.3, 2026-10-22): the session keeps its last input, the action of the command, a click, a fill, a type, a press, a swipe or a scroll, and the time the mirror injected that command's last input, also when the command failed after it went in (`handlers::noting_input`). A settle within 1 s of it first waits for a change of the tree, or of the frames unless `--tree-only`, after that instant, one that came before the wait ending it at once, at most until 1 s after the input; the mirror's `settle` then counts the quiet window, its predicate unchanged, and `--timeout` bounds that part alone; `elapsedMs`, `treeChanges` and `frameChanges` cover both waits through `Mirror::changes`. The reply names the input's action in `input`, and `endedBy` the signal that changed last since the input, `none` when nothing did, which the line says as `nothing changed within 1 s of the click`, `the typing` for a type; without such an input the settle counts its quiet window alone. `open` notes its window change before the launch and settles the same way from it, within the explicit timeout when one is given, so an app already in front that the fake shows again 300 ms after the launch settles at about 600 ms where it settled at 201 ms. On the fake at a 300 ms latency the settle right after `click #tab-list` answered `✓ Settled after 401.71 ms: frames changed last`, and after a tap on empty space `✓ Settled after 793.41 ms: nothing changed within 1 s of the click`. On this session's Simulator a settle right after the Enter that loads a page in a browser answered `Settled after 1036.07 ms: frames changed last`, then 1512.46, 1552.89 and 900.36 ms on three more pages, where the demo pass had 0.01 ms and nothing changed, and the settle right after the tap that opens the browser's menu (1283.87 ms, frames) was followed by a listing of the menu's buttons where the demo pass had listed none.

### D3. A stale ref resolves to its twin
When a ref's element is gone from the replica, the daemon looks for its twin in the replica as it is: an element of the same role and identifier, else one of the same role, name and bounds within the slack the reach computation uses; it acts on the twin, the reply's `resolved` naming the new ref and the line saying `(@e221 now @e240)`, and the session's refs gain the twin. Without a twin the failure stays `stale_ref` with its advice. Name, role and bounds together keep a list's rows apart (a row that scrolled into the same bounds has another name); identifier and role alone suffice where the app gives them, as task 5.5's read-back found. As built (task 2.2, 2026-10-22): `Mirror::twin` takes, for a stale ref, the one element bearing a ref, outside a keyboard, of the role and identifier the ref was issued for, else the one of its role and name whose bounds lie edge by edge within a physical pixel of those a listing last gave it, the slack the reach computation takes an edge with; none when no element, or more than one, matches so. The ref table keeps the element's identifier, set as the ref is issued, and its bounds in pixels, at each listing, beside its role, name and place; the stale ref stays stale and the twin carries a ref of its own. The daemon resolves a stale ref so wherever an action or a query names one, `click`, `fill`, `type`, `get` and `scroll`'s target, the session's refs gaining the twin's ref, and the reply carries `resolved` (`reply::Resolved`), `{"stale": "@e2", "ref": "@e4"}`, with the twin's ref as `ref` where the reply has one; the line reads `✓ Done (@e2 now @e4, hit textbox "placeholder-textbox-1", ...)` and a get's `(@e3 now @e5)` after its value; a wait on a ref still waits on the ref's own element. The map app's case was not a ref stale before the call, as the demo pass read it: on this session's Simulator a fill by the search field's ref, taken right after `open`'s snapshot, failed `stale_ref` three times in three because its own tap, which readies a field on iOS, raised the search card and put the field in a new node before the text went in, while a get on the ref right after resolved to the twin by identifier. So `fill` follows its field to the twin before its text goes in and before it enters the text again in one piece, which the fake's `raised` board gives, a field that moves from 580 to 120 points in a view of another class as it takes the focus; with it the same fill answered `✓ Done (@e4 now @e20, read back "coffee", typed)` three times in three. A field the text set rebuilds on Android, after its entry, is the read-back's to find, as task 5.5 of `real-app-fixes` left it.

### D4. `open` leaves the app in front
After its launch and settle, `open` checks the replica's foreground owner (the `origin` the listing reports) is the app's package; when it is not and the app is running (the case after `press home`), the driver activates it (on the Simulator a second launch of a running app brings it to the front where the first did not; the task measures which call does) and `open` checks again; when the app still is not in front, `open` fails with `not_in_front` naming what is, instead of answering opened. The Android driver's launch of a running activity brings it to the front already; the check costs one read of the replica. As built (task 2.4, 2026-10-23): measured on this session's Simulator through the CLI, a second `open` of the settings app after `press home` and `wait --settle` brought the running app to the front six times in six, the driver's launch (CoreSimulator's launch call, which `simctl launch` makes) activating it. The demo pass's case came from an `open` that met the home screen's go-home animation, as one right after `press home` does, and as the demo pass's settle, which ended at once before task 2.3, let it: that `open` showed the app's screen in 70 to 177 ms, settled and answered opened, while the animation's end gave the home screen the front back, three times in three; a second launch, through the driver or `simctl launch`, then brought the app to the front three times in three each. So `open`, once the app's screen showed and settled with no dialog of system UI in front, reads the replica's foreground owner (`origin`) and, when another holds the front, launches the app again, with no new driver call, waits at most 2 s for its screen within the explicit timeout, settles again from that launch and reads the front again, failing `not_in_front`, naming the app and what is in front, when the app still is not; an app that showed no screen within its wait, as a slow one behind the launcher, keeps its warning, since the launcher in front of it is no app taken back. The fake's home returns (`FakeDriver::with_home_returns`) give the case, the launcher taking the front back 150 ms after the first launches of a running app made from it, red with `open` answering opened and settled with SpringBoard in front, then green on both platforms; on the Simulator `press home` then `open` at once answered with the settings app in front five times in five, right after and 2 s later, the second launch's screen 1.86 to 1.92 s after the call, and with a settle between, twice in two with no second launch.

### D5. Delta lines
The human output of a delta whose kind is `delta` prints a header line, `delta (revision 3 from 2): lines 2 to 4 replaced by`, then the delta's lines as the listing prints them, and the `removedRefs` as the full snapshot prints them; `--json` keeps agent-browser's object byte for byte. The server's result text follows, since it is the CLI's output. As built (task 1.1, 2026-10-22): `crates/cli/src/output.rs` counts the base revision's lines from 1 where the object's `startLine` counts from 0: `line 2 replaced by` for one line, `lines 2 to 4 replaced by` for several, `inserted after line 4`, or `before line 1`, for lines that replace none, and `lines 3 to 4 removed` for lines left without a replacement; then `treeChange`'s lines as they come, and `removed refs: @e5, @e6` from the delta's `remove` changes, in the `@eN` form of a snapshot's `removedRefs`, of which the full snapshot's human output prints none, so the line is the delta's own; the refs a delta adds or renames show in its lines. A change wait prints its delta the same way, an unknown kind keeps the pretty JSON, and `--json` is untouched. On the fake's Login tab a fill and a Back gave `delta (revision 2 from 1): line 2 replaced by` and the username's line through the CLI and the server, where both gave the object as pretty JSON.

### D6. A plain scroll answers once the content has come to rest
A plain `scroll` strokes as a finger that lifts moving (`Release::Moving`), as a swipe does, so the content glides on after its strokes, and its reply came once the strokes went in: in the replay through the server a click right after `scroll down 700` and `scroll up 700` in Photos named `hit button "Favorites"` and opened nothing three times in three, the list still gliding taking the tap as a stop, and opened the album three times in three with a settle between (`reports/12.3-action-verdicts.md`, finding 1). After its last stroke the plain scroll waits for the screen to come to rest with the mirror's settle, which a scroll that looks for an element runs after each of its strokes, then reads the tree once more, so that the next command aims at where the content stopped; the reply says whether the screen came to rest (`settled`) and how long the content moved on after the last stroke's lift (`movedOnMs`), and the line reads `✓ Done (2 strokes, at rest 2341.3 ms after the last)`. One rule, which names no app: the glide is the platform's. Alternatives: strokes that come to rest before the finger lifts, as `--until`'s do (kept for `--until`, whose looks between strokes must not miss the element; a plain scroll keeps the fling a flick gives and says how long it lasted); a warning on a click dispatched while the frames still change (rejected: by then the list has the tap, and a click cannot tell a glide from an animation). As built (task 3.4, 2026-10-23): measured first on this session's Simulator, with a settle right after a plain scroll, the content moved on after the reply for 0.7 to 1.0 s where the glide met the list's end (settles of 893 to 1,204 ms, frames changing last, on Settings' root and its Accessibility and Privacy & Security lists), for 2.3 to 2.5 s on a list of 85 rows (2,515 to 2,671 ms after one stroke of 300 points over 300 ms, on General's Dictionary), and on Photos' Collections after `scroll down 700` for 2.2 to 2.5 s and once past 2.8 s, as a flinging 600-point stroke took 2.8 to 2.9 s to settle on the Simulator in the foundation's design D8; a click at the screen's centre right after `scroll down 300` on Settings' root opened nothing three times in three, the list stopping short of where it came to rest, and opened Privacy & Security three times in three with a settle between, and a tap 1.5 s into the long list's glide still stopped it, 23.7 points short of its rest. So the settle is bounded by the settle's default maximum, 3 s (`GLIDE_SETTLE`), where the 1.5 s `--until` gives a stroke (`UNTIL_SETTLE`) would answer mid-glide; `movedOnMs` runs from the mirror's time for the last stroke to the last change of the frames or the tree the settle saw, 0 when nothing changed after it, and a screen still changing at 3 s answers `settled: false`, the line `still moving 3000.0 ms after the last` and a warning to settle before a click. On the fake, `--fake-fling` glides the fixture's list as long as its platform's fling lasts, Android's spline duration and UIKit's deceleration down to 10 points a second, slowing evenly, a frame and the tree showing each move, and a tap on the list while it glides stops it and reaches nothing; red on the reply without `settled` and on the Android fake's row 16 left unopened by a click right after the scroll, then green on both platforms, a list that moves as far as the finger answering one quiet window after its stroke. On the Simulator `click text=Favorites` right after `scroll down 700` and `scroll up 700` opened the album three times in three, the scrolls answering at rest 1,876.5 to 2,598.0 ms after their last stroke but for two `scroll down 700` still moving at 3 s, Photos' Collections changing on, and a click at the centre right after `scroll down 300` on Settings' root opened Privacy & Security three times in three, the scroll at rest 911.6 to 933.9 ms after its stroke; on the list of 85 rows the scroll answered at rest 2,298.3 and 2,647.2 ms after its stroke.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [The hit element from the replica differs from the app's hit-test] → the warning says the replica's view; the next listing is the truth; no refusal on a mismatch.
- [A twin resolves to the wrong element] → identifier and role first; name, role and bounds together otherwise; the reply names the resolution so a model can see it.
- [The settle's first-change wait adds up to 1 s where nothing was going to change] → only within 1 s of an input, and the reply says nothing came, which ends the model's guessing.
- [A plain scroll takes as long as the glide it starts, up to 3 s, and a screen that keeps changing holds it the whole 3 s] → the glide is time a click right after would have lost; the reply says how long the content moved on, or that the screen was still changing, with a warning.
