# 10.20: What each architecture costs the host and the device

Task 10.20 of `mobile-automation-foundation` asks what each tool's architecture costs the host and the device: the resident memory and CPU of mobile-agent (daemon, reader, agent), Maestro 2.10.0 (JVM, driver) and agent-device 0.21.14 (daemon, runner, bridge), while idle and across ten back-to-back runs of the login flow, on the iOS Simulator and on the Android emulator, sampled every 0.5 s with the process trees named.

This report records the Simulator run of 2026-10-07, from 03:01 to 03:22 UTC. Times are UTC. The raw samples are not committed; the tables below summarize them.

- **mobile-agent** keeps a daemon of 11.5 MB on the host and a reader of 11.7 MB in the Simulator. Idle, the reader's tree polls cost 0.36 CPU-s per minute, and the fixture app spends 1.01 CPU-s per minute answering them, against 0.02 or less under the other tools. A flow costs it 0.35 CPU-s: 0.25 in the daemon, 0.08 in the reader and 0.02 in its CLI processes.
- **agent-device** keeps six processes resident: its node daemon (89 to 108 MB), the runner's `xcodebuild` (100 MB), the runner app in the Simulator (35 MB), the snapshot bridge and its `simctl spawn` (20 MB), and Xcode's `DTServiceHub`. A flow costs 2.75 CPU-s, 7.9 times mobile-agent's, most of it in the XCTest runner app and its `xcodebuild`.
- **Maestro** keeps nothing resident: its JVM, its driver's `xcodebuild` and the driver app end with each `maestro test`. A flow costs 18.82 CPU-s, 54 times mobile-agent's. Of that, 3.90 CPU-s goes to the Android SDK's `avdmanager`, a JVM that Maestro starts four or five times per flow on an iOS Simulator. Each flow peaks at 544 MB.
- **The device.** Each flow relaunches the app, and the Simulator's own processes and rendering services work for every tool; at rest they use about 0.2 of a core. Counting the app and the rest of the Simulator, a flow costs the host about 9.7 CPU-s through mobile-agent, 17.7 through agent-device and 43.9 through Maestro, background included.

### Method

- **Memory: the physical footprint.** Every host figure is the process's physical footprint, `ri_phys_footprint` from `proc_pid_rusage(RUSAGE_INFO_V4)`, the figure `top` prints as MEM, Activity Monitor as Memory and `footprint` as Footprint. Before the run, the three agreed on two processes: Finder at 104 MB and the Dock at 67 MB.
  - It counts the memory the kernel charges to the process alone: its dirty private pages, anonymous or file-backed, including compressed and swapped ones, and the IOKit and graphics memory it owns.
  - It leaves out clean pages the process shares with others, such as the dyld shared cache and mapped binaries.
  - `ps -o rss` counts every resident page a process maps, shared ones included, and leaves out compressed ones. The reader in the Simulator had an RSS of about 120 MB against a footprint of 12 MB, because RSS counts the Simulator runtime's shared libraries, and the fixture app 330 MB against 51 MB. The Dock had 25 MB of RSS against 67 MB of footprint, most of its memory compressed. A sum of RSS over a tool's processes would count the same shared pages once per process.
  - The same definition applies to every tool and every host process, including those in the Simulator, which are host processes. MB is 2^20 bytes, as `top` and `footprint` print it.
  - A tool's peak and mean are taken over the sum of its live processes' footprints at each sample, so its peak is that sum's highest value, not the sum of each process's peak.
- **CPU: accumulated time at the window's edges.** Each process's CPU time, `ri_user_time + ri_system_time` converted from Mach ticks with the timebase, is read at every sample and interpolated to the window's start and end; a process born inside the window counts from zero. CPU-seconds come from these accumulated times, never from averaged percentages. Three refinements make the figures exact where the record allows:
  - The harness's commands (`mobile-agent` CLI processes, `maestro test`, `agent-device batch`) take their exact CPU, their own and their reaped descendants', from the harness's reaped-children time (`ri_child_user_time + ri_child_system_time` of `mobile-agent-bench`), read every 20 ms as each command ends. All 115 commands of the run were caught.
  - A child its parent reaps inside the window, such as each `simctl` or `avdmanager` that Maestro's JVM runs, takes the jump of the parent's reaped-children time at the sample where it went, and none of its own samples, so nothing is counted twice. A jump no sampled child claims is listed as helpers too short for a sample.
  - A process in the Simulator that exits, such as Maestro's driver app after each flow, loses the CPU between its last sample and its exit, unless it was the only child of the Simulator's `launchd_sim` to exit at that sample: then it takes the jump of `launchd_sim`'s reaped-children time.
  - The rows of each table add up to the tool's total within 0.013 CPU-s per window. The largest difference is in agent-device's idle window: its daemon reaped a `pgrep`, which had started 0.28 s before the window, 0.24 s into it. That `pgrep`'s row takes its whole CPU less what it had used at the window's start, while the total spreads it evenly over the daemon's half-second sampling interval; the record cannot tell which share of its 0.03 CPU-s fell inside the window.
- **Sampler.** A Python script using only the standard library, which reads `proc_pidinfo` and `proc_pid_rusage` through `ctypes`. Every 0.5 s it records the timestamp, the load average and, for every process on the host (1,480 to 1,760 of them), its pid, ppid, footprint, RSS, CPU and reaped-children CPU. Each process's command line is recorded once, when the process is first seen. The script itself took 2.9 to 3.2 CPU-s per minute, 5 % of one core, and 14 MB.
- **Process trees named.** Each process is given to a tool by its command and by its ancestry when first seen. Ancestry alone does not suffice: daemons leave their parent, and processes in the Simulator are children of its `launchd_sim`. The groups:
  - mobile-agent: `mobile-agent` CLI processes, `mobile-agent-daemon` and `simulator-reader-*`, which runs in the Simulator.
  - Maestro: the JVM running `maestro.cli` and every process it starts, its driver's `xcodebuild test-without-building` and Xcode's `DTServiceHub` under it, and `maestro-driver-iosUITests-Runner` in the Simulator.
  - agent-device: its node client and daemon, the daemon's children, `xcodebuild … AgentDeviceRunnerUITests …`, `AgentDeviceRunnerUITests-Runner` and `snapshot-bridge` in the Simulator, and the `simctl spawn` that hosts the bridge.
  - The device: the rest of the target Simulator's `launchd_sim` tree, and its rendering services, one `SimRenderServer` and three `SimMetalHost`, which are children of `launchd` and are told from other Simulators' by their start in the same second as its `launchd_sim`. The fixture app is shown apart, and `testmanagerd`, which XCTest's runners use, is shown within the tree.
- **Windows.**
  - **Ten runs.** The harness ran `run --target <udid> --cli target/release/mobile-agent --fixture swiftui --flow login --iterations 10 --warmup 1`: golden checks through each tool, then each tool's series with the harness's hand-offs between them. A tool's window runs from the first command of its first measured iteration, after the warm-up, to the end of the last command of its tenth, as the harness's commands started and ended. Each window is 4 to 20 ms longer than the sum of the ten iterations' durations in the harness's `report.json`, the time between iterations.
  - **Idle.** After the harness run, each tool ran one login flow with the commands the harness runs and its flow files. Then came 60 s with the tool attached and no command running, the fixture showing "Welcome, agent", and then the harness's release: for mobile-agent `status` and `shutdown`, for agent-device `close` and `daemon stop --clean`. The window starts when the flow's last command has returned.
- **Load.** The harness run and each idle window's warm-up flow started only after the 1-minute load had stayed under 4 for 60 s, polled every 5 s. The tools' own work raised the load inside the windows (see [Windows](#windows)): the golden checks ran just before mobile-agent's series, and Maestro's processes used up to 2.9 cores over a half-second sample, 2.2 at the 95th percentile. Idle windows began 10 to 25 s after their warm-up flow, which the 1-minute average still carries.

## Findings

1. **What is resident.** Attached and idle on the Simulator, mobile-agent holds 23 MB in two processes, agent-device 248 MB on average in six, and Maestro nothing of its own. agent-device's two largest processes, its node daemon and the runner's `xcodebuild` at about 100 MB each, are the cost of keeping an XCTest runner warm between commands; mobile-agent's reader, the guest process that takes the runner's place for reading, is 11.7 MB.
2. **What runs when idle.** mobile-agent and agent-device cost about the same while idle, 0.42 and 0.46 CPU-s per minute, under 1 % of a core. mobile-agent's idle cost is the reader's tree poll, and it makes the app work too: 1.01 CPU-s per minute in the fixture, which no other tool caused. Maestro costs nothing between flows, because it keeps nothing.
3. **What a flow costs the host.** 0.35 CPU-s through mobile-agent, 2.75 through agent-device and 18.82 through Maestro. Per flow, agent-device's XCTest runner app and its `xcodebuild` cost 1.85 CPU-s, five times mobile-agent's whole flow. Maestro pays for a JVM start, a driver install and an `xcodebuild` per flow, and spends 3.90 CPU-s on Android SDK JVMs that an iOS run does not need.
4. **What a flow costs the device.** The Simulator's own processes and rendering services work more under the XCTest-based tools: about 5.7 CPU-s per flow beyond their background under mobile-agent, 8.6 under agent-device and 17.4 under Maestro, whose figure includes `testmanagerd` and its driver's reinstall. The app's own CPU per flow was 2.70 to 2.82 s under Maestro and mobile-agent and 4.69 s under agent-device.
5. **Peaks follow processes started per flow.** Maestro's footprint swings from nothing to 544 MB within each flow, as its JVM, the driver's `xcodebuild` and an `avdmanager` JVM overlap; agent-device's and mobile-agent's peaks during the runs are about 60 MB and 2 MB above their idle means.
