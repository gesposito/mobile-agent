# 10.20: What each architecture costs the host and the device

Task 10.20 of `mobile-automation-foundation` asks what each tool's architecture costs the host and the device: the resident memory and CPU of mobile-agent (daemon, reader, agent), Maestro 2.10.0 (JVM, driver) and agent-device 0.21.14 (daemon, runner, bridge), while idle and across ten back-to-back runs of the login flow, on the iOS Simulator and on the Android emulator, sampled every 0.5 s with the process trees named.

This report records two sessions of 2026-10-07: the Simulator's from 03:01 to 03:22 UTC, and the emulator's from 04:14 to 04:33 UTC. A first emulator session, from 03:41 to 04:05, was set aside ([below](#a-first-emulator-session-set-aside)). Times are UTC. The raw samples are not committed; the tables below summarize them, and the scripts that took and summarized them are in `reports/10.20-resources/` ([Rerun](#rerun)).

- **mobile-agent** keeps a daemon on the host, 11.5 MB beside the Simulator and 17.7 MB beside the emulator, and one process on the device: the reader in the Simulator (11.7 MB) or the agent in the guest (69 MB PSS). It is the one tool that keeps reading the screen while idle: the reader's polls cost 0.36 CPU-s per minute and the fixture 1.01 answering them; on the emulator the agent's poll costs 0.33 in the guest and the fixture 0.75. A flow costs it 0.35 CPU-s on the Simulator, and 0.28 on the host plus 0.05 in the guest on the emulator.
- **agent-device** keeps an XCTest runner warm on the Simulator: six processes, 248 MB, its node daemon and the runner's `xcodebuild` at about 100 MB each. A flow costs 2.75 CPU-s there, 7.9 times mobile-agent's, most of it in the runner app and its `xcodebuild`. On Android it keeps its daemon (85 MB) and a helper instrumentation in the guest (84 MB PSS, 66 MB of it the `am instrument` process that hosts the helper); idle it costs almost nothing, and a flow costs 0.62 CPU-s on the host and 0.22 in the guest.
- **Maestro** keeps nothing resident: its JVM and its driver are started, and on both platforms the driver reinstalled, for every `maestro test`. A flow costs 18.82 CPU-s on the Simulator, 54 times mobile-agent's, and 13.45 on the host plus 1.82 in the guest on the emulator. On both it runs the other platform's tools every flow: the Android SDK's `avdmanager`, a JVM, four or five times on an iOS run (3.90 CPU-s per flow), and on an Android run `avdmanager` (3.20) and Xcode's `simctl`, `devicectl` and `xcodebuild` (1.04). Each flow peaks at 544 MB on the Simulator, and at 364 MB on the host plus 151 MB in the guest on the emulator.
- **The device.** Each flow relaunches the app, and the device works for every tool. The Simulator's own processes and rendering services rest at about 0.2 of a core; counting the app and the rest of the Simulator, a flow costs the host about 9.7 CPU-s through mobile-agent, 17.7 through agent-device and 43.9 through Maestro, background included. The emulator's guest rests at 0.09 of a vCPU, and its CPU is host CPU: counting the emulator process, a flow costs the host 5.2 CPU-s through mobile-agent, 7.6 through agent-device and 31.3 through Maestro, background included.

### Method

- **Memory on the host: the physical footprint.** Every host figure is the process's physical footprint, `ri_phys_footprint` from `proc_pid_rusage(RUSAGE_INFO_V4)`, the figure `top` prints as MEM, Activity Monitor as Memory and `footprint` as Footprint. Before the run, the three agreed on two processes: Finder at 104 MB and the Dock at 67 MB.
  - It counts the memory the kernel charges to the process alone: its dirty private pages, anonymous or file-backed, including compressed and swapped ones, and the IOKit and graphics memory it owns.
  - It leaves out clean pages the process shares with others, such as the dyld shared cache and mapped binaries.
  - `ps -o rss` counts every resident page a process maps, shared ones included, and leaves out compressed ones. The reader in the Simulator had an RSS of about 120 MB against a footprint of 12 MB, because RSS counts the Simulator runtime's shared libraries, and the fixture app 330 MB against 51 MB. The Dock had 25 MB of RSS against 67 MB of footprint, most of its memory compressed. A sum of RSS over a tool's processes would count the same shared pages once per process.
  - The same definition applies to every tool and every host process, including those in the Simulator, which are host processes. MB is 2^20 bytes, as `top` and `footprint` print it.
  - A tool's peak and mean are taken over the sum of its live processes' footprints at each sample, so its peak is that sum's highest value, not the sum of each process's peak.
- **Memory in the emulator's guest: PSS.** A guest process's figure is its proportional set size, `Pss` in `/proc/<pid>/smaps_rollup`: its private pages plus, for each page it shares, its share of that page among the processes that map it. It is the figure `dumpsys meminfo` reports as TOTAL PSS: on the running Expo fixture, `smaps_rollup` read 68,109 kB and `dumpsys meminfo` 68,119 kB.
  - `dumpsys meminfo <pid>` was not sampled every 0.5 s. Each call made the measured process itself spend 18 ms of CPU, and `system_server` 16 ms, which at 0.5 s would have added about 4 % of a core to the very CPU being measured. Reading `smaps_rollup` puts no work on the measured process.
  - Guest PSS and host footprint are different definitions; the report never adds one to the other.
- **CPU: accumulated time at the window's edges.** Each process's CPU time, `ri_user_time + ri_system_time` converted from Mach ticks with the timebase, is read at every sample and interpolated to the window's start and end; a process born inside the window counts from zero. CPU-seconds come from these accumulated times, never from averaged percentages. Three refinements make the figures exact where the record allows:
  - The harness's commands (`mobile-agent` CLI processes, `maestro test`, `agent-device batch`) take their exact CPU, their own and their reaped descendants', from the harness's reaped-children time (`ri_child_user_time + ri_child_system_time` of `mobile-agent-bench`), read every 20 ms as each command ends. All 115 commands of the Simulator run were caught, and 104 of the emulator run's 105, the miss being the harness's last `adb` read as it exited. A command caught between its fork and its exec takes its command line from the 0.5 s scan.
  - A child its parent reaps inside the window, such as each `simctl` or `avdmanager` that Maestro's JVM runs, takes the jump of the parent's reaped-children time at the sample where it went, and none of its own samples, so nothing is counted twice. A jump no sampled child claims is listed as helpers too short for a sample.
  - A process in the Simulator that exits, such as Maestro's driver app after each flow, loses the CPU between its last sample and its exit, unless it was the only child of the Simulator's `launchd_sim` to exit at that sample: then it takes the jump of `launchd_sim`'s reaped-children time.
  - The rows of each table add up to the tool's total within 0.013 CPU-s per window. The largest difference is in agent-device's idle window on the Simulator: its daemon reaped a `pgrep`, which had started 0.28 s before the window, 0.24 s into it. That `pgrep`'s row takes its whole CPU less what it had used at the window's start, while the total spreads it evenly over the daemon's half-second sampling interval; the record cannot tell which share of its 0.03 CPU-s fell inside the window.
- **Guest CPU: `/proc/<pid>/stat`.** A guest process's CPU is its `utime` plus `stime`, in ticks of 1/100 s accumulated since it started, read every 0.5 s and taken at the window's edges as on the host. The guest's busy time is the sum of `user`, `nice`, `system`, `irq` and `softirq` in `/proc/stat`.
- **Sampler, host side.** `reports/10.20-resources/sampler.py`, a Python script using only the standard library, which reads `proc_pidinfo` and `proc_pid_rusage` through `ctypes`. Every 0.5 s it records the timestamp, the load average and, for every process on the host (1,490 to 1,790 of them), its pid, ppid, footprint, RSS, CPU and reaped-children CPU. Each process's command line is recorded once, when the process is first seen. The script itself took 2.3 to 3.2 CPU-s per minute, 4 to 5 % of one core, and 14 MB.
- **Sampler, guest side.** `reports/10.20-resources/guest.sh`, a shell loop run as root in the guest through `su 0`, which the userdebug image allows, over one `adb shell`, its lines stamped by the host as they arrive. Every 0.5 s it reads the CPU and PSS of the tools' processes and the fixture, which it finds by name every 2 s; every 2 s it also lists every guest process's accumulated CPU (`ps -A -o TIME+`), so that work outside the tools' processes can be attributed. The loop took 4.4 to 5.0 CPU-s per minute, 7 to 8 % of one of the guest's four vCPUs, mostly in those listings; the guest's busy time below leaves it out.
- **Process trees named.** Each process is given to a tool by its command and by its ancestry when first seen. Ancestry alone does not suffice: daemons leave their parent, and processes in the Simulator are children of its `launchd_sim`. The groups:
  - mobile-agent: `mobile-agent` CLI processes, `mobile-agent-daemon` and `simulator-reader-*`, which runs in the Simulator; in the guest, the agent, `app_process … dev.mobileagent.agent.Main`, which names itself `mobile-agent-0.4.1-…`.
  - Maestro: the JVM running `maestro.cli` and every process it starts, its driver's `xcodebuild test-without-building` and Xcode's `DTServiceHub` under it, and `maestro-driver-iosUITests-Runner` in the Simulator; in the guest, its driver, the instrumented `dev.mobile.maestro` process, and the `am instrument -w` that runs it.
  - agent-device: its node client and daemon, the daemon's children (on Android, `adb` commands), `xcodebuild … AgentDeviceRunnerUITests …`, `AgentDeviceRunnerUITests-Runner` and `snapshot-bridge` in the Simulator, and the `simctl spawn` that hosts the bridge; in the guest, `com.callstack.agentdevice.snapshothelper`, the `am instrument -w` that runs it, and `com.callstack.agentdevice.imehelper`.
  - The device: the rest of the target Simulator's `launchd_sim` tree, and its rendering services, one `SimRenderServer` and three `SimMetalHost`, which are children of `launchd` and are told from other Simulators' by their start in the same second as its `launchd_sim`. The fixture app is shown apart, and `testmanagerd`, which XCTest's runners use, is shown within the tree. On the emulator, the host's `qemu-system-aarch64-headless` process, and apart the adb server, which all three tools used.
- **Windows.**
  - **Ten runs.** The harness ran `run --target <id> --cli target/release/mobile-agent --fixture <swiftui or compose> --flow login --iterations 10 --warmup 1`: golden checks through each tool, then each tool's series with the harness's hand-offs between them. A tool's window runs from the first command of its first measured iteration, after the warm-up, to the end of the last command of its tenth, as the harness's commands started and ended. Each window is 4 to 22 ms longer than the sum of the ten iterations' durations in the harness's `report.json`, the time between iterations.
  - **Idle.** After the harness run, each tool ran one login flow with the commands the harness runs and its flow files. Then came 60 s with the tool attached and no command running, the fixture showing "Welcome, agent", and then a release: for mobile-agent `status` and `shutdown`, for agent-device `close` and `daemon stop --clean`, as the harness does on a Simulator. The window starts when the flow's last command has returned. On the emulator, the idle windows began after agent-device's daemon from the harness run had exited on its own.
- **Load.** The harness run and each idle window's warm-up flow started only after the 1-minute load had stayed under 4 for 60 s, polled every 5 s. The tools' own work raised the load inside the windows (see each device's Windows): the golden checks ran just before mobile-agent's series, and Maestro's processes used up to 2.9 cores of the host over a half-second sample, 2.2 at the 95th percentile. Each idle window began as its warm-up flow, which took 4 to 25 s, returned, so the 1-minute average at its start still carries that flow.

## Findings

1. **What is resident.** Attached and idle, mobile-agent holds 23 MB in two processes on the Simulator, and on the emulator 17.7 MB on the host and a 69 MB PSS agent in the guest. agent-device holds 248 MB in six processes on the Simulator, and on the emulator 87 MB on the host and 84 MB PSS in the guest. Maestro holds nothing on either. agent-device's largest items on iOS, its node daemon and the runner's `xcodebuild` at about 100 MB each, are the cost of keeping an XCTest runner warm between commands; mobile-agent's reader, the guest process that takes the runner's place for reading, is 11.7 MB. In the Android guest the two resident tools are close: an instrumentation needs an `am instrument -w` process of 66 MB PSS beside its helper, and mobile-agent's agent is one `app_process` of the same kind, 69 MB.
2. **What runs when idle.** mobile-agent keeps reading the screen while idle, by design: on the Simulator the reader's poll costs 0.36 CPU-s per minute and makes the app spend 1.01, which no other tool caused; on the emulator the agent's poll costs 0.33 in the guest and the app 0.75, the daemon 0.11 on the host, and the guest and the emulator process ran 3.3 and 3.7 CPU-s per minute above rest. agent-device costs 0.46 CPU-s per minute on the Simulator, its daemon and XCTest runner ticking over, and about 0.05 on Android. Maestro costs nothing between flows, because it keeps nothing. The tools' own processes stay under 1 % of a core while idle; counting the work it causes, mobile-agent's idle cost is about 2.4 % of a core on the Simulator (with the app's) and 6 % on the emulator (with the emulator process's extra).
3. **What a flow costs the host.** The tools' own processes: 0.35 CPU-s through mobile-agent, 2.75 through agent-device and 18.82 through Maestro on the Simulator; 0.28, 0.62 and 13.45 on the emulator. Per flow, agent-device's XCTest runner app and its `xcodebuild` cost 1.85 CPU-s on the Simulator, five times mobile-agent's whole flow; on Android its host side is small, its work being in `adb` commands and the guest's helper. Maestro pays for a JVM start and a driver install per flow, and runs the other platform's tools every time: Android SDK JVMs on iOS (3.90 CPU-s per flow), and on Android `avdmanager` (3.20) and Xcode's tools (1.04).
4. **What a flow costs the device.** On the Simulator, its own processes and rendering services work about 5.7 CPU-s per flow beyond their background under mobile-agent, 8.6 under agent-device and 17.4 under Maestro, whose figure includes `testmanagerd` and its driver's reinstall; the app's own CPU per flow was 2.70 to 2.82 s under Maestro and mobile-agent and 4.69 s under agent-device. In the emulator's guest a flow costs about 4.5 CPU-s beyond rest under mobile-agent, 6.4 under agent-device and 14.9 under Maestro. The tools' own guest processes take 0.05, 0.22 and 1.82 of it, and the app 2.39, 2.86 and 3.48. Under Maestro, `system_server` adds 1.78 CPU-s per flow as its driver is installed and started again, and Gboard 0.69 as it types.
5. **The emulator's guest is host CPU.** The emulator process's CPU follows the guest's busy time, so the device's cost lands on the host too: counting it, a flow cost the host 5.2 CPU-s through mobile-agent, 7.6 through agent-device and 31.3 through Maestro, background included.
6. **Peaks follow processes started per flow.** Maestro's footprint swings from nothing to 544 MB within each flow on the Simulator, as its JVM, the driver's `xcodebuild` and an `avdmanager` JVM overlap, and to 364 MB on the host and 151 MB PSS in the guest on the emulator. mobile-agent's peaks during the runs are 2 MB (Simulator) and 10 MB (emulator) above its idle means, agent-device's 58 and 65 MB.
7. **One emulator session's guest saturated and rebooted** during Maestro's series, while the tools' processes and the fixture used 0.14 of a vCPU between them; what used the guest was not recorded, and the rerun, which recorded every guest process, saw neither ([A first emulator session, set aside](#a-first-emulator-session-set-aside)).

## Rerun

**Reproduce.** The scripts behind these figures are in `reports/10.20-resources/`, standard-library Python 3 and one shell loop, each with a header that says what it does and how to run it: `sampler.py` records the host every 0.5 s and, given an emulator, runs `guest.sh` in its guest; `drive.py` runs a device's session around the sampler; `analyze.py` turns a session's directory into the figures and tables above. The raw samples stay out of the repository. From the repository root, in order:

1. Build. The daemon embeds the Android agent, so the Android toolchain must be on `PATH`, as must agent-device and Maestro (on the measured host, `eval "$(mise env)"`):

   ```sh
   cargo build --release
   ```

2. Boot the device. The Simulator:

   ```sh
   xcrun simctl boot 7B292E1E-7A29-41A5-9C69-2ABA1CEB3683
   xcrun simctl bootstatus 7B292E1E-7A29-41A5-9C69-2ABA1CEB3683 -b
   ```

   Or the emulator, then its reset from `reports/10.1-emulator.md`, Rerun (the stock keyboard selected, agent-device's input method disabled, its helpers stopped, the launcher in front):

   ```sh
   emulator -avd pixel_api36 -no-window -no-audio -no-boot-anim -gpu host -grpc 8554 -grpc-use-token &
   ```

3. Sampler, harness and guest loop: one command per device. `drive.py` starts `sampler.py`, which on the emulator starts `guest.sh` through `adb shell su 0`, waits until the 1-minute load has stayed under 4 for 60 s, runs the harness, then each tool's idle window, and stops the sampler:

   ```sh
   python3 reports/10.20-resources/drive.py --device sim --out <sim dir>
   python3 reports/10.20-resources/drive.py --device emu --out <emu dir>
   ```

   Its defaults are the published runs': the Simulator above or `emulator-5554` (`--target`), `MOBILE_AGENT_DRIVERS` from the device, `$MOBILE_AGENT_SOCKET_DIR` and `$AGENT_DEVICE_STATE_DIR` or fresh short directories under `/tmp` (`--socket-dir`, `--state-dir`), and on the emulator a wait of up to 330 s after the harness for agent-device's daemon (task 10.27). The same steps by hand, each in its own shell:

   ```sh
   python3 reports/10.20-resources/sampler.py --out <dir>/samples --sim-udid 7B292E1E-7A29-41A5-9C69-2ABA1CEB3683
   python3 reports/10.20-resources/sampler.py --out <dir>/samples --emulator emulator-5554   # with the guest loop
   target/release/mobile-agent-bench run --target <udid or emulator-5554> --cli target/release/mobile-agent \
     --fixture <swiftui or compose> --flow login --iterations 10 --warmup 1 --out <dir>/run
   adb -s emulator-5554 push reports/10.20-resources/guest.sh /data/local/tmp/res-guest.sh   # the guest loop alone
   adb -s emulator-5554 shell "su 0 sh /data/local/tmp/res-guest.sh 0.5 4 'mobile-agent|maestro|agentdevice|dev.mobileagent.fixture|instrument'"
   ```

   By hand, the idle windows follow the harness one tool at a time, with the marks `drive.py` writes to `<dir>/marks.jsonl` (`<tool>-idle-start`, `<tool>-idle-end`): the tool's login flow with the harness's commands and flow files, 60 s with no command, the release ([Method](#method)).

4. Analysis:

   ```sh
   python3 reports/10.20-resources/analyze.py <dir>            # every window, process by process
   python3 reports/10.20-resources/analyze.py --tables <dir>   # this report's tables
   ```

   The ten-run windows come from the harness's commands as the sampler saw them start and end, checked against the iterations in `<dir>/run/report.json`.
