# 10.17: list-scroll through `scroll --until`

Task 10.17 has the harness's list-scroll flow for `mobile-agent` scroll to `row-200` as an agent does, with one `scroll --timeout 90000 --until id=row-200 -- down 600`, in place of the CLI adapter's own loop of `wait --settle`, a zero-time `wait` on the row and `scroll -- down 600` (commit 3da516f, `fix(bench): list-scroll scrolls with one scroll --until, as an agent does`). The flow's other steps, its golden (`detail-200` present) and its timing, from the first command's start to the last one's end, are unchanged. Commit dd4ed5a (`feat(bench): run --flow measures the named reference flows alone`) let each run below measure this flow alone.

This file records the list-scroll series before and after the change on the Android emulator with its controller on, and before it on the Simulator. The Simulator's numbers after it come with the full Simulator harness run of task 10.2, in `reports/10.2-simulator/`. The bench's own reports are under `reports/10.17-list-scroll/`; none of them names a home directory, so nothing in them was rewritten to `$HOME`.

Every series passed its golden check and had a 0.0 % failure rate; every one but SwiftUI's before had a 0.0 % flake rate.

- **Emulator, the scroll.** Before, each iteration of either fixture scrolled in 8 steps of the adapter's loop, 26 commands: 9 settles, 9 zero-time checks and 8 plain `scroll`s. After, it sent one `scroll --until`, whose reply counted 16 strokes and gave `visible` 1.0 in all 53 iterations of each fixture: `row-200` was whole in view when the scroll stopped. A plain 600-point stroke lifts moving and flings these lists 1,103 to 1,319 points, where `scroll --until`'s strokes come to rest before the lift, so that a 600-point one moves them 554 to 592 points, and carry them at most three quarters of what they show (task 10.16, design D8): twice the strokes, each moving the list about half as far.
- **Emulator, the time.** The flow is 1.46 s faster at p50 on Expo and 1.48 s on Compose, and 1.05 and 1.42 s faster at p95, with twice the strokes. Where the time went is not broken down here; design D8 measured the settle after a flinging 600-point stroke at 1,027 to 1,260 ms on this emulator against 192 to 302 ms after a resting one, and the flow sends one command for the scroll where it sent 26.
- **Emulator, against 10.1.** The before numbers are within 136 ms (Expo) and 118 ms (Compose) of 10.1's at p50. At p95 Expo's is 780 ms lower and Compose's within 14 ms, and 10.1's 24.6 s maximum on Expo did not recur.
- **Simulator, before.** The adapter's loop took SwiftUI's list in 7 steps in 40 iterations and in 6 in 10 (medians 25.371 and 23.476 s), and Expo's in 6 in all 50. SwiftUI flaked in 8 of 50 iterations, each passing on its retry. In 5 the loop had stopped with `row-200`'s centre below the 874-point screen, so with less than half of the row on it, and the click was refused as off the screen, at (201, 875.3) to (201, 889). In 3 the click was sent and `Detail 200` did not appear within 10 s. That is the stop on any overlap that task 10.15 replaced in `scroll --until` with its mostly-in-view rule, which the flow now uses.
- **Load.** The bench records the load at each run's start. Polled every few minutes during the runs, the 1-minute load read between 2.2 and 4.0 through both emulator runs and the first 30 minutes of the Simulator run. From about 14:55 UTC to that run's end at 15:10, macOS's XProtect remediator scans took it to between 4.7 and 11.7, while Expo's series, the run's last, was under way; its iterations took 20.34 to 21.11 s from the first to the last, with no drift.
