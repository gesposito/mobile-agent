# 10.4: React Native smoke test on the Simulator and the emulator

Task 10.4 of `mobile-automation-foundation` asks for a smoke test of a Bluesky-class React Native app on the Simulator and the emulator, verifying that `mobile-agent`'s snapshots are not degraded on screens where an XCTest-based tool reports sparse roots. Design D6 expects that of the spawned reader, and agent-device's ADR 0004 (`docs/adr/0004-ios-snapshot-backend-strategy.md` at the commit `docs/research/simulator.md` pins) names Bluesky as the app whose screens XCTest's recursive snapshot reduces to "no useful child nodes". This report records three runs, the numbers and the verdict: on 2026-09-27 the first (Setup to Other findings) on Ignite's demo app, the Expo fixture and Bluesky's Android release, after Bluesky's iOS install failed under Socket Firewall, and the second ([Second run](#second-run-bluesky-1133-on-the-simulator)) on Bluesky's own Simulator build, logged out; on 2026-10-06 the third ([Third run](#third-run-signed-in-bluesky-1133)) on the screens behind Bluesky's sign-in, on the Simulator and the emulator.

## Verdict

The claim is verified: on the screens where agent-device's XCTest tree came back sparse or failed, `mobile-agent`'s snapshots were whole and not degraded.

- Logged out, the condition never arose. On none of the 17 React Native screens of the first two runs on the Simulator (9 Bluesky, 5 Ignite, 3 Expo fixture) did an XCTest-based snapshot report a sparse root: agent-device's XCTest runner, forced to its recursive tree, returned a healthy tree on every screen.
- Signed in (third run), it arose on two Bluesky screens on the Simulator. On the Search tab with the keyboard up, the forced XCTest tree timed out in all 3 reads (the typeahead for "nobel", twice, and the search history): in interactive mode agent-device returned no nodes and classed the read `sparse` ("No snapshot backend could read this screen"), and in raw mode its runner fell back to its private AX backend (164 and 162 nodes). On a thread with 21 replies (711 nodes), both raw reads timed out the same way. On those 5 screen reads `mobile-agent` was not degraded: 26 refs and 48 lines on the typeahead, its 8 accounts, the field's text and the keyboard among them; 18 refs and 68 lines on the history; 26 refs and 65 lines on the thread.
- Everywhere else XCTest's tree was healthy: 44 of the 52 forced reads across 26 screen reads in two passes, each an Application root with its 2 windows, 3 with the keyboard, and 101 to 601 nodes 34 to 50 levels deep. No read returned the root without useful children that ADR 0004 describes; the failures were the runner running out of its 1 s budget for preparing the tree, which turned on tree size and window count: the same typeahead read healthy in the second pass in 1.05 to 1.25 s.
- `mobile-agent` was not degraded on 25 of the 26 signed-in Simulator screen reads and on all 11 emulator screens, where agent-device's helper read every screen healthy as well. The one degraded verdict was `sibling_collision`, for a carousel's identical "View video" links on the Discover feed after six strokes. Across the runs it never reported a sparse tree. The second run's `truncated` verdicts with the keyboard up did not recur, since the reader counts the keyboard's keys as delivered (`crates/simulator/README.md`, Tree), and the first run's `sibling_collision` on the native Settings app shows the verdict is live.
- The third run found three defects (third run, findings 1 to 3; tasks 10.21 to 10.23): with the keyboard up on iOS every element counts as 0% in view, so `click` warns wrongly and, by the same measure, `scroll --until` cannot find its element in view; an overlay the app marks modal leaves what it covers in `snapshot -i`; and `fill` once reported success while the field held other text. The second run found that a daemon built without the reader deadlocks the Simulator's worker instead of falling back to the translator (second run, finding 1).
- agent-device 0.21.14 reads local Simulators through its AX bridge, a guest reader spawned into the Simulator as ours is (request-log producer `simulator-ax-bridge`). In the first two runs XCTest served it only as a fallback, once, when forced, and for `--actions`; signed in to Bluesky, its default reads went through XCTest until SafariViewService, which the sign-in had started, was terminated (third run, finding 5).

## Setup

- Host: Apple M1 Max, macOS 26.6.2, Xcode 27.0 (27A266a), CoreSimulator 1171.7, iOS 26.5 runtime. Other work's builds ran throughout; the 1-minute load average ranged from about 8 to 240 during the runs, so every time below is indicative only.
- Simulator: `rn-smoke`, an iPhone 17 created for this test with `xcrun simctl create`. `AccessibilityEnabled`, `ApplicationAccessibilityEnabled` and `AutomationEnabled` were written into its `com.apple.Accessibility` preferences with `plutil` before the first boot, as the driver's `create_device` does. It booted headless in 49 s and was deleted afterwards; DeviceHub.app and Simulator.app never ran. Two other booted Simulators were listed by the daemon but never attached.
- Emulator: `emulator-5554` (API 36, arm64, shared with other work). The Android steps ran while no other automation was active on it; it was left on the launcher.
- `mobile-agent`: release build of `cli` and `daemon` at `e4fbcc5`, with the Android agent jar from `agent-android/build.sh`. Each platform's daemon had its own socket directory, `MOBILE_AGENT_DRIVERS` set to that platform's driver and `MOBILE_AGENT_DEVICE` naming the target. This build reports no read time for a snapshot: there is no `readMs` in the reply.
- agent-device 0.21.14 (npm global), with its own state directory. Three routes to a Simulator snapshot exist:
  - `snapshot -i` and `snapshot --raw`, which its daemon serves from the Simulator AX bridge (`apple/snapshot-bridge`, started in the Simulator with `simctl spawn`), falling back to XCTest per app launch.
  - The XCTest runner's recursive `XCUIElement` snapshot, the `tree` backend. The CLI and the typed client do not expose it; `scripts/agent-device-tree-snapshot.mjs` requests it with the daemon flag `snapshotPreferredBackend`, and the reply's `snapshotQuality` confirms the backend (`tree`, reason code `requested-backend` in interactive mode).
  - `snapshot --actions`, which bypasses the bridge but makes the runner pick its private AX backend rather than the tree; it was tried on Settings only.
- Per screen, in this order: `mobile-agent wait --settle`, `snapshot -i` twice, `snapshot`, `screenshot`; then agent-device `snapshot -i` and `snapshot --raw` with `--cost --level full --debug`; then the tree helper in `i` and `raw` modes. Navigation used `mobile-agent click` on refs from the preceding snapshot, or on the selectors the tables name; agent-device only read.

## Apps and builds

### Bluesky 1.133 for the Simulator: the first install

`bluesky-social/social-app` at tag `1.133.0` (`2cd7a11250a0cd3d0b10bc3bc95feb6d57a0629a`, released 2026-09-23): Expo SDK 57.0.24, React Native 0.86.3. The repository moved from yarn to pnpm between releases 1.120, which still has `yarn.lock`, and 1.125, and its `devEngines` asks for pnpm 11.23.0 and Node 24.19. pnpm 11.23.0 came through `sfw npx`; Node 24.19.0 was already installed. The clone took 7 s; the install was abandoned after 55 minutes of attempts.

`pnpm install --frozen-lockfile` under `sfw` fetched and linked all 1,727 packages within its first seven runs, but every run then died in the lockfile's supply-chain verification. With `trustPolicy: no-downgrade` in `pnpm-workspace.yaml`, pnpm re-checks each of the 1,726 lockfile entries against the package's full registry metadata, 64 requests at a time, a number `--network-concurrency` does not govern. Socket Firewall's proxy opened many more upstream connections than pnpm had open, and kept them: 35 s into a run its process held 442 established connections to the registry and 44 in `SYN_SENT`, while pnpm held its 50 local tunnels, and machine-wide the established connections to port 443 climbed by about 80 every 4 s to about 930. New connections then stalled, sfw's lookups at its own firewall API hit their 10 s connect timeout, and sfw ended the run (exit 137). This happened at load averages from 8 to 200. Bursts of 64 metadata requests, direct or through sfw, completed in about 3 s, so the failure is the verification's sustained volume through the proxy, not a single burst.

Tried, without changing the outcome: `--network-concurrency=4`; `maxsockets` 4 (through `pnpm_config_maxsockets`); retries with `--prefer-offline`, which reuse metadata already cached (the verification's cache grew from 545 to 575 packages over the last seven runs, so retries do not converge); and Node's connection-attempt options for the sfw binary (`--network-family-autoselection-attempt-timeout=3000`, `--no-network-family-autoselection`). pnpm's `--trust-lockfile` skips the verification; the run's own permission policy refused it as a weakening of the install's security, and it was not pursued further. Building Bluesky for the Simulator therefore needed a host or network on which sfw passes that verification, or an explicit decision to install with `--trust-lockfile`; the second run took the latter (see [Second run](#second-run-bluesky-1133-on-the-simulator)).

### Ignite 11.5 demo app for the Simulator: the fallback

The brief's fallback is another large open-source Expo app that builds cleanly. Ignite's boilerplate, the demo app `ignite-cli` generates, has a sign-in screen, a welcome screen and four tabs (a component showroom whose tree has 313 elements, a podcast list loaded from the network, community, debug), and needs no account or secret. It is Expo SDK 55.0.5 and React Native 0.83.2, two SDKs below the fixture's 57. Bundle identifier `com.rnsmoke`. Steps, in a scratch directory outside the repository:

1. `sfw npx -y ignite-cli@11.5.0 new RnSmoke --yes --packager=bun --git=false --workflow=cng --installDeps=true`. The project was generated, but its bun install died in sfw the same way as pnpm's verification, twice, after 41 and 288 packages.
2. `sfw bun install --network-concurrency=8`: 1,089 packages in 31 s. Bun blocked one postinstall, `unrs-resolver`'s, an ESLint resolver the build does not use.
3. `sfw bunx expo prebuild -p ios --no-install`.
4. `pod install`: 102 pods in 76 s.
5. `xcodebuild -workspace ios/RnSmoke.xcworkspace -scheme RnSmoke -configuration Release -destination 'generic/platform=iOS Simulator' -derivedDataPath build CODE_SIGNING_ALLOWED=NO build`. It compiled in 6 min 17 s, then failed in the bundle phase: Xcode had resolved `PROJECT_DIR` through the `/tmp` symlink, while Metro's root was the real path it points to, so Metro could not resolve the entry file. The phase honors a preset `ENTRY_FILE`, and the rerun with `ENTRY_FILE` set to the entry file's real path built in 31 s.

From the first generation attempt to the `.app` took 18 minutes.

## Other findings

1. Named containers without traits get no ref. Ignite's email and password fields are React Native views marked accessible with only a label, so the `TextInput` inside is not in the tree for either tool. agent-device's `-i` lists the two containers (as `Cell "Email"`, `Cell "Password"`), while `mobile-agent` lists them as `generic "Email"` and `generic "Password"` in the full snapshot with no ref, so its `-i` on the sign-in screen shows only "Tap to log in!". The spec's `-i` keeps interactive roles and named content roles; these nodes are neither, although XCTest reports them hittable.
2. A scroll amount below the touch slop is a tap. `scroll` takes its amount in points, as agent-browser's takes pixels, so `scroll down 2` made one 2-point stroke through the screen's center. The app took it as a tap on the podcast card under the center, which opened the episode's audio in Safari. A minimum stroke, or a refusal of amounts below the slop, would avoid acting on whatever lies under the center.
3. On Android, a sheet reissues the refs of the screen under it. While Bluesky's hosting-provider sheet was up, the sign-in screen was hidden from accessibility (its elements were absent from the listing); after the sheet closed they came back with new refs, and `click @e3` failed with `stale_ref`, whose message led to the new ref. This is D5's rule that refs are reissued when an element's identity disappears.
4. On the emulator, the first click after attaching reported no `frameChangeMs` although the screen changed: its first tree delta came at 1,322.7 ms and the watch ended with it, past the 1 s bound. Later clicks reported both spans.
5. Start-up under load. The first command of the test, `devices`, gave up with "the daemon did not answer within 30 s" while the load average was near 200; the daemon it had started answered the next command. The reader was ready 342.4 ms after its spawn on that daemon's first read and 933.9 ms after it on a later daemon's, and dtuhidd's first session of the boot activated in 849.6 ms, while the load average was above 60 and peaked at 240; spikes S4 and S3 measured 292 ms and 270 to 330 ms.
6. Foreground policy differs. When Safari covered the app, `mobile-agent` read Safari and named it as `origin`; agent-device's runner reactivated the session's app before answering and stopped using its bridge for that app launch.

### Findings

1. A daemon built without the reader deadlocks instead of falling back to the translator.
   - Cause: the first build came right after the toolchain pin (`f6e9505`), when Rust 1.95.0 had no `aarch64-apple-ios-sim` standard library; the target was added to that toolchain later the same morning. So the build script embedded no reader and warned that "tree reads will use the host translator".
   - Deadlock: on the first read, `TreeSlot::reader` (`crates/simulator/src/macos/reader.rs`) holds its state lock while calling the spawn closure. `SimulatorDriver::spawn_reader` (`crates/simulator/src/macos/driver.rs`) finds no embedded reader and calls `TreeSlot::give_up`, which takes the same lock, so the worker thread blocks for good. A sample of the daemon showed `TreeSlot::reader`, `spawn_reader`, `give_up`, `Mutex::lock`.
   - Effect: every command for the Simulator failed after 30 s with `daemon_timeout`, and `status` listed it as not attached; the daemon was stopped with SIGTERM. The translator fallback the warning promises never ran.
2. The keyboard makes the verdict `truncated`, with the wrong advice.
   - On the three screens whose text field had the focus, and on none of the other six, the verdict was degraded as `truncated`, advising that "the read stopped at its node count limit; raise the limit or read a narrower part of the screen". The listing ends with the keyboard's window and no keys.
   - The reader marks a node-count cut whenever the snapshot reports more children than it delivered (`crates/simulator/reader/src/elements.rs`). The keyboard's content lives in its own process, and agent-device's bridge reads it as a second application. No limit was reached: the listings had 22 to 25 lines, and the limit is 10,000 nodes.
3. Covered controls keep their refs. Under the hosting-provider sheet, the birth-date dialog and the language selector, `snapshot -i` still listed the screen beneath with 7 to 9 refs, although each dialog sits in its own window over that screen. The sheet and the birth-date dialog put a full-window backdrop labelled "dismiss popup" (`PopoverDismissRegion`) over it. Both agent-device reads presented only the dialog.
4. Labelled controls without traits get no ref, as Ignite's fields did in the first run. The sheet's Automatic and Manual options and its Dismiss control, the birth-date field and the 16 languages listed were `generic` lines in `snapshot` and absent from `snapshot -i`; agent-device lists them (as `Other` and `Cell`). Selectors reach them (`#manualSelectBtn`, `text=Date of birth`), but the interactive listing of the language selector shows only the covered form behind it.
5. `open` settles on the launch splash, as the previous section describes, so the first snapshot after a launch lists no interactive element with a clean verdict. That is accurate, but an agent has to wait for an element after `open`.
6. A ref moved to another control. `e6` named the "Forgot password?" button of the sign-in form and, after that click, the "Next" button of the forgot-password form, which the mirror took for the same element, as its identity (window, class, identifier and sibling path) allows when neither has an identifier. A command reusing `e6` from the earlier snapshot would press "Next" rather than fail with `stale_ref`.

### Findings

1. With the keyboard up on iOS, every element counts as 0% in view.
   - `click #loginNextButton` on the sign-in form, and `click @e341` on the search history's "Search for nobel" row, 215 to 259 points down the screen, warned that the element "was not mostly in view (0% of it in view)", while both sat above the keyboard, whose own element begins 583 points down, and both taps did what they aimed at: the first signed in, the second ran the search. No click warned with the keyboard down. Two clicks on `#bottomBarHomeBtn`, which the keyboard does cover, hit its globe key instead, switching the keyboard's language and back; the second, whose reply was kept, warned the same way, rightly.
   - Cause: the reader gives the keyboard's input-method window the bounds of `UIRemoteKeyboardWindow`, the whole screen (XCTest and the bridge read all three windows at 402 by 874 points), and the mirror cuts an element's visible bounds by every system or input-method window above its own (`bars` in `crates/mirror/src/visibility.rs`), so the keyboard's window leaves nothing. `scroll --until` stops by the same measure (`crates/cli/README.md`, Commands), so it cannot find its element in view while the keyboard is up; that was not tried.
   - On the emulator the sign-in click warned the same way, but on the search screen with the input method up a click and `scroll --until` on the typeahead's "Search for" row found it whole (`visible` 1.0, no stroke). There the input method showed only a floating toolbar, so the Android case is not established.
2. An overlay the app marks modal does not cut what it covers.
   - Bluesky shows its search typeahead and history as a view with `accessibilityViewIsModal` over the Explore page or the results (`src/screens/Search/Shell.tsx` at `1.133.0`). 3. `fill` reported success while the field held other text. 6. `wait --settle` stopped at its 3 s bound on the screens where something kept playing: frames still changing on the first pass's scrolled Discover feed and thread, under an animated GIF, and frames and the tree on the emulator's Discover feed after six strokes, under a playing video and its countdown. Elsewhere it ended within 2,377 ms, mostly at once.
7. The one degraded verdict, `sibling_collision` on the Discover feed after six strokes, named two pairs of a "Trending videos" carousel's "View video" links, which differ only by position: the verdict as designed.

### What the findings led to

Findings 1 to 3 became tasks 10.21 to 10.23, fixed on 2026-10-07 and checked on this Simulator with the app still signed in.

- **Finding 1 (10.21).** The reader bounds the keyboard's window by what it draws: the input set host view, 539 to 874 of the screen's 874 points on iOS 26.5, since the `Keyboard` element alone (583 to 816) leaves out the prediction bar above it. On the search screen with the keyboard up, `click` on Cancel search and on a history row warned no more, and `scroll --until` on a typeahead row ended after 0 strokes with the row whole (`visible` 1.0) where it had failed with `element_not_found`. The emulator's case is now established and is task 10.25: Gboard's floating toolbar gives the input-method window the box around its touch region, (0, 898) to (1080, 2400) pixels, so anything below 342 dp counts as out of view there.
- **Finding 2 (10.22).** No snapshot attribute reports `accessibilityViewIsModal` (none of the 81 XCTAutomationSupport knows), so the reader asks the app to honour modal views as XCTest's queries do (`snapshotKeyHonorModalViews`, free while no view is modal), reads again without the option when that left children out (41 ms more on this screen) and flags the views the first read kept; the mirror hides the siblings' nodes within the view's bounds and keeps those outside, among them Bluesky's header with the search field, which React Native leaves a sibling of the typeahead. Refs under the search history went from 18 to 10, under the typeahead over Explore from 28 to 22 and over results from 35 to 17, and a results ref taken before the typeahead opened fails with `element_covered`.
- **Finding 3 (10.23).** `fill` reads the field back, holds a match for 200 ms, enters the text again in one piece when the field shows other text, pasted on iOS, and fails with `text_mismatch` naming what the field holds. Eight fills of "nobel" into the search field all read back "nobel"; the first found "nobe" after the keys and pasted instead, with the warning naming it (`readBackMs` 843), the other seven took the keys alone (`readBackMs` 348 to 849 ms, the keyboard up throughout). On the fixtures the read-back adds 250 to 290 ms to a fill, almost all of it the hold (`READ_BACK_HOLD` in `crates/daemon/src/handlers/act.rs`), so the text-entry and login series need measuring again (task 10.26).
