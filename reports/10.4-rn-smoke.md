# 10.4: React Native smoke test on the Simulator and the emulator

Task 10.4 of `mobile-automation-foundation` asks for a smoke test of a Bluesky-class React Native app on the Simulator and the emulator, verifying that `mobile-agent`'s snapshots are not degraded on screens where an XCTest-based tool reports sparse roots. Design D6 expects that of the spawned reader, and agent-device's ADR 0004 (`docs/adr/0004-ios-snapshot-backend-strategy.md` at the commit `docs/research/simulator.md` pins) names Bluesky as the app whose screens XCTest's recursive snapshot reduces to "no useful child nodes". This report records two runs on 2026-09-27, the numbers and the verdict: the first (Setup to Other findings) on Ignite's demo app, the Expo fixture and Bluesky's Android release, after Bluesky's iOS install failed under Socket Firewall; the second ([Second run](#second-run-bluesky-1133-on-the-simulator)) on Bluesky's own Simulator build.

## Verdict

The claim is still not verified, and the task stays open.

- Bluesky 1.133.0 was built for the Simulator in a second run, after the install ran with `--trust-lockfile` on the user's decision, and tested on the 9 screens the logged-out app reaches (see [Second run](#second-run-bluesky-1133-on-the-simulator)). No test account was available, so the signed-in screens (feeds, threads, profiles), where ADR 0004 and agent-device issue #1105 place XCTest's failures, were not reached.
- On none of the 17 React Native screens tested on the Simulator (9 Bluesky, 5 Ignite, 3 Expo fixture) did an XCTest-based snapshot report a sparse root. agent-device's XCTest runner, forced to its recursive tree, returned a healthy tree on every screen, so the condition the claim is about never arose.
- `mobile-agent` never reported a sparse tree. Its verdict was not degraded on 14 of the 17 Simulator screens, nor on the 4 Bluesky screens on the emulator. On the 3 Bluesky screens with the keyboard up it was degraded as `truncated`, which its advice attributes to the node-count limit; the cut came with the keyboard, whose content lives in its own process (second run, finding 2). The first run's one other degraded verdict, `sibling_collision` on the native Settings app, shows the verdict is live.
- agent-device 0.21.14 no longer uses XCTest for local Simulator snapshots by default: every `snapshot -i` and `snapshot --raw` here came from its Simulator AX bridge (request-log producer `simulator-ax-bridge`, no runner round trips), a guest reader spawned into the Simulator as ours is. XCTest served only as its fallback, once, when forced, and for `--actions`.
- The second run also found that a daemon built without the reader deadlocks the Simulator's worker instead of falling back to the translator (second run, finding 1).
- Verifying the claim needs a signed-in Bluesky account on the Simulator.

## Setup

- Host: Apple M1 Max, macOS 26.6.2, Xcode 27.0 (27A266a), CoreSimulator 1171.7, iOS 26.5 runtime. Other agents' builds ran throughout; the 1-minute load average ranged from about 8 to 240 during the runs, so every time below is indicative only.
- Simulator: `rn-smoke`, an iPhone 17 created for this test with `xcrun simctl create`. `AccessibilityEnabled`, `ApplicationAccessibilityEnabled` and `AutomationEnabled` were written into its `com.apple.Accessibility` preferences with `plutil` before the first boot, as the driver's `create_device` does. It booted headless in 49 s and was deleted afterwards; DeviceHub.app and Simulator.app never ran. Two other booted Simulators were listed by the daemon but never attached.
- Emulator: `emulator-5554` (API 36, arm64, shared with other agents). The Android steps ran while no other automation was active on it; it was left on the launcher.
- `mobile-agent`: release build of `cli` and `daemon` at `d827124`, with the Android agent jar from `agent-android/build.sh`. Each platform's daemon had its own socket directory, `MOBILE_AGENT_DRIVERS` set to that platform's driver and `MOBILE_AGENT_DEVICE` naming the target. This build reports no read time for a snapshot: there is no `readMs` in the reply.
- agent-device 0.21.14 (npm global), with its own state directory. Three routes to a Simulator snapshot exist:
  - `snapshot -i` and `snapshot --raw`, which its daemon serves from the Simulator AX bridge (`apple/snapshot-bridge`, started in the Simulator with `simctl spawn`), falling back to XCTest per app launch.
  - The XCTest runner's recursive `XCUIElement` snapshot, the `tree` backend. The CLI and the typed client do not expose it; `scripts/agent-device-tree-snapshot.mjs` requests it with the daemon flag `snapshotPreferredBackend`, and the reply's `snapshotQuality` confirms the backend (`tree`, reason code `requested-backend` in interactive mode).
  - `snapshot --actions`, which bypasses the bridge but makes the runner pick its private AX backend rather than the tree; it was tried on Settings only.
- Per screen, in this order: `mobile-agent wait --settle`, `snapshot -i` twice, `snapshot`, `screenshot`; then agent-device `snapshot -i` and `snapshot --raw` with `--cost --level full --debug`; then the tree helper in `i` and `raw` modes. Navigation used `mobile-agent click` on refs from the preceding snapshot, or on the selectors the tables name; agent-device only read.

## Apps and builds

### Bluesky 1.133 for the Simulator: the first install

`bluesky-social/social-app` at tag `1.133.0` (`2cd7a11250a0cd3d0b10bc3bc95feb6d57a0629a`, released 2026-09-23): Expo SDK 57.0.24, React Native 0.86.3. The repository moved from yarn to pnpm between releases 1.120, which still has `yarn.lock`, and 1.125, and its `devEngines` asks for pnpm 11.23.0 and Node 24.19. pnpm 11.23.0 came through `sfw npx`; Node 24.19.0 was already installed. The clone took 7 s; the install was abandoned after 55 minutes of attempts.

`pnpm install --frozen-lockfile` under `sfw` fetched and linked all 1,727 packages within its first seven runs, but every run then died in the lockfile's supply-chain verification. With `trustPolicy: no-downgrade` in `pnpm-workspace.yaml`, pnpm re-checks each of the 1,726 lockfile entries against the package's full registry metadata, 64 requests at a time, a number `--network-concurrency` does not govern. Socket Firewall's proxy opened many more upstream connections than pnpm had open, and kept them: 35 s into a run its process held 442 established connections to the registry and 44 in `SYN_SENT`, while pnpm held its 50 local tunnels, and machine-wide the established connections to port 443 climbed by about 80 every 4 s to about 930. New connections then stalled, sfw's lookups at its own firewall API hit their 10 s connect timeout, and sfw ended the run (exit 137). This happened at load averages from 8 to 200. Bursts of 64 metadata requests, direct or through sfw, completed in about 3 s, so the failure is the verification's sustained volume through the proxy, not a single burst.

Tried, without changing the outcome: `--network-concurrency=4`; `maxsockets` 4 (through `pnpm_config_maxsockets`); retries with `--prefer-offline`, which reuse metadata already cached (the verification's cache grew from 545 to 575 packages over the last seven runs, so retries do not converge); and Node's connection-attempt options for the sfw binary (`--network-family-autoselection-attempt-timeout=3000`, `--no-network-family-autoselection`). pnpm's `--trust-lockfile` skips the verification; this session's permission policy refused it as a weakening of the install's security, and it was not pursued further. Building Bluesky for the Simulator therefore needed a host or network on which sfw passes that verification, or an explicit decision to install with `--trust-lockfile`; the second run took the latter (see [Second run](#second-run-bluesky-1133-on-the-simulator)).

The brief's fallback is another large open-source Expo app that builds cleanly. From the first generation attempt to the `.app` took 18 minutes.

## Other findings

1. Named containers without traits get no ref. Ignite's email and password fields are React Native views marked accessible with only a label, so the `TextInput` inside is not in the tree for either tool. agent-device's `-i` lists the two containers (as `Cell "Email"`, `Cell "Password"`), while `mobile-agent` lists them as `generic "Email"` and `generic "Password"` in the full snapshot with no ref, so its `-i` on the sign-in screen shows only "Tap to log in!". The spec's `-i` keeps interactive roles and named content roles; these nodes are neither, although XCTest reports them hittable.
2. A scroll amount below the touch slop is a tap. `scroll` takes its amount in points, as agent-browser's takes pixels, so `scroll down 2` made one 2-point stroke through the screen's center. The app took it as a tap on the podcast card under the center, which opened the episode's audio in Safari. A minimum stroke, or a refusal of amounts below the slop, would avoid acting on whatever lies under the center.
3. On Android, a sheet reissues the refs of the screen under it. While Bluesky's hosting-provider sheet was up, the sign-in screen was hidden from accessibility (its elements were absent from the listing); after the sheet closed they came back with new refs, and `click @e3` failed with `stale_ref`, whose message led to the new ref. This is D5's rule that refs are reissued when an element's identity disappears.
4. On the emulator, the first click after attaching reported no `frameChangeMs` although the screen changed: its first tree delta came at 1,322.7 ms and the watch ended with it, past the 1 s bound. Later clicks reported both spans.
5. Start-up under load. The first command of the test, `devices`, gave up with "the daemon did not answer within 30 s" while the load average was near 200; the daemon it had started answered the next command. The reader was ready 342.4 ms after its spawn on that daemon's first read and 933.9 ms after it on a later daemon's, and dtuhidd's first session of the boot activated in 849.6 ms, while the load average was above 60 and peaked at 240; spikes S4 and S3 measured 292 ms and 270 to 330 ms.
6. Foreground policy differs. When Safari covered the app, `mobile-agent` read Safari and named it as `origin`; agent-device's runner reactivated the session's app before answering and stopped using its bridge for that app launch.

### XCTest reads and sparse roots

All 18 XCTest tree reads, 9 screens in both modes, returned a healthy tree: none was sparse and none failed. The bridge's 18 reads were healthy as well. The two backends' raw trees had the same node count on 4 screens, and XCTest's was smaller on 5:

- On sign-in (123 against 186), XCTest's tree lacked the keyboard, which the bridge read as a second application (`UIRemoteKeyboardWindow`, 38 keys). On the other two keyboard screens XCTest included it (35 keys).
- Under the hosting-provider sheet, its Manual tab, the birth-date dialog and the language selector (54, 58, 46 and 114 against 155, 159, 174 and 245), XCTest's tree held only the dialog's windows, while the bridge also read the covered screen. Both presented only the dialog in interactive mode.

agent-device's runner names the birth-date picker's wheels `Element(39)` and the date picker itself `Element(51)`, the XCUIElementType values of pickerWheel and datePicker; its bridge names them `PickerWheel` and `DatePicker`.

Right after a launch, the screen is Expo's static launch splash: `open` settles in about 0.7 s, and `snapshot` lists only `image [id=SplashScreenLegacy]`, not degraded, until the landing appears a few seconds later. Two relaunches polled both tools through that period. With the load average at 20 to 30 and reads taking 1 to 15 s, every XCTest read completed after the landing had rendered (70 and 81 nodes, healthy), so this run did not observe XCTest during the splash itself.

### Findings

1. A daemon built without the reader deadlocks instead of falling back to the translator.
   - Cause: the first build came right after the toolchain pin (`3bbbbb0`), when Rust 1.95.0 had no `aarch64-apple-ios-sim` standard library; the target was added to that toolchain later the same morning. So the build script embedded no reader and warned that "tree reads will use the host translator".
   - Deadlock: on the first read, `TreeSlot::reader` (`crates/simulator/src/macos/reader.rs`) holds its state lock while calling the spawn closure. `SimulatorDriver::spawn_reader` (`crates/simulator/src/macos/driver.rs`) finds no embedded reader and calls `TreeSlot::give_up`, which takes the same lock, so the worker thread blocks for good. A sample of the daemon showed `TreeSlot::reader`, `spawn_reader`, `give_up`, `Mutex::lock`.
   - Effect: every command for the Simulator failed after 30 s with `daemon_timeout`, and `status` listed it as not attached; the daemon was stopped with SIGTERM. The translator fallback the warning promises never ran.
2. The keyboard makes the verdict `truncated`, with the wrong advice.
   - On the three screens whose text field had the focus, and on none of the other six, the verdict was degraded as `truncated`, advising that "the read stopped at its node count limit; raise the limit or read a narrower part of the screen". The listing ends with the keyboard's window and no keys.
   - The reader marks a node-count cut whenever the snapshot reports more children than it delivered (`crates/simulator/reader/src/elements.rs`). The keyboard's content lives in its own process, and agent-device's bridge reads it as a second application. No limit was reached: the listings had 22 to 25 lines, and the limit is 10,000 nodes.
3. Covered controls keep their refs. Under the hosting-provider sheet, the birth-date dialog and the language selector, `snapshot -i` still listed the screen beneath with 7 to 9 refs, although each dialog sits in its own window over that screen. The sheet and the birth-date dialog put a full-window backdrop labelled "dismiss popup" (`PopoverDismissRegion`) over it. Both agent-device reads presented only the dialog.
4. Labelled controls without traits get no ref, as Ignite's fields did in the first run. The sheet's Automatic and Manual options and its Dismiss control, the birth-date field and the 16 languages listed were `generic` lines in `snapshot` and absent from `snapshot -i`; agent-device lists them (as `Other` and `Cell`). Selectors reach them (`#manualSelectBtn`, `text=Date of birth`), but the interactive listing of the language selector shows only the covered form behind it.
5. `open` settles on the launch splash, as the previous section describes, so the first snapshot after a launch lists no interactive element with a clean verdict. That is accurate, but an agent has to wait for an element after `open`.
6. A ref moved to another control. `e6` named the "Forgot password?" button of the sign-in form and, after that click, the "Next" button of the forgot-password form, which the mirror took for the same element, as its identity (window, class, identifier and sibling path) allows when neither has an identifier. A command reusing `e6` from the earlier snapshot would press "Next" rather than fail with `stale_ref`.
