# 10.26: text entry and the login flow again after 10.23

Task 10.26 of `mobile-automation-foundation` asks for the series that `fill` takes part in to be measured again. Since task 10.23, `fill` reads the field back and takes the text only once the field still shows it 200 ms after the entry (`READ_BACK_HOLD` in `crates/daemon/src/handlers/act.rs`), which the published tables predate. Two series fill a field: the text-entry primitive and the login flow, the one reference flow with a `fill` step (`crates/bench/src/flow.rs`; list-scroll and big-screen have none). The task also asks whether the hold is worth its cost against a read-back without it.

This report records the runs of 2026-10-07 on the Simulator and the emulator of the published runs, against the same baselines, and two more runs per device of mobile-agent alone, one with the hold as built and one without a hold. Once task 10.30 had kept the hold for text typed as keys alone, mobile-agent's emulator series ran once more, the same day ([10.30 rerun](#1030-rerun)), once task 10.29 had the mirror read a field's window again as soon as a pasted text's frame changes, its Simulator series ([10.29 rerun](#1029-rerun)), and once task 10.31 had a paste set the pasteboard without `devicectl`, its Simulator text entry and SwiftUI login ([10.31 rerun](#1031-rerun)). Times are UTC. The harness's reports are under `reports/10.26-remeasure/`.

## Verdict

- **Simulator, text entry.** 545.5 ms at p50 on SwiftUI and 718.3 ms on Expo, against 249.3 and 228.3 ms published (`reports/10.2-simulator.md`). Two things add to it:
  - the read-back, 283.2 and 262.6 ms of each fill at the median;
  - an entry that now falls in two clusters. The time before the read-back starts was 172 to 289 ms in 165 of the 300 measured text entries of the three Simulator runs, as long as the published entries took in all, and 426 to 559 ms in the other 135. The run without the hold has both clusters, so they are not the hold. The series' p50 lands in either cluster: 515.3 to 718.3 ms over the three runs.
- **Simulator, login.** 4,173.0 ms at p50 on SwiftUI and 3,860.2 ms on Expo, against 3,817.1 and 3,706.8: 355.9 ms (9.3 %) and 153.4 ms (4.1 %) more. That is less than the flow's two read-backs, 619.2 and 546.0 ms at their medians.
- **Emulator, text entry.** 224.1 ms on Expo and 240.0 ms on Compose, against 23.8 and 26.1 ms published with the controller on (`reports/10.1-emulator.md`). The read-back is 201.0 and 218.6 ms of it.
- **Emulator, login.** 1,969.3 ms on Expo and 3,567.0 ms on Compose, against 1,577.6 and 3,370.0: 391.7 ms (24.8 %) and 197.0 ms (5.8 %) more.
- **Baselines.** At p50, mobile-agent's login was faster than:
  - Maestro 2.10.0, by 6.10 (SwiftUI) and 6.45 (Expo) on the Simulator, against 6.62 and 6.64, and by 11.89 (Expo) and 6.60 (Compose) on the emulator, against 14.50 and 5.99. Maestro's own Compose login took 23.5 s, against 20.2 s published (finding 4).
  - agent-device 0.21.14, by 2.11 on SwiftUI, against 2.27, and by 2.14 on the Simulator's Expo fixture, where the published run did not time it and this one timed 5 of 50 iterations (finding 3). On the emulator, by 2.33 (Expo, 24 % failed and 12 % flaky) and 1.67 (Compose), against 2.02 and 1.74; agent-device's Expo median moved between two clusters of its own (finding 3).
- **Fills.** 424 fills per run, 2,544 over the six runs, every timed iteration's, warm-ups included. None was retried and none carried a warning. `readBackMs` at the median was 263 to 337 ms on the Simulator, 201 ms on the emulator's Expo fixture and 219 to 230 ms on its Compose fixture.
- **The hold.** Without it, the read-back took the text as soon as a read showed it that began after the entry, or after which nothing had changed.
  - On the emulator's Expo fixture, whose React Native fields report a change by event at once, the hold was almost the whole read-back: 201 ms per fill with it and 18 to 20 ms without. Without it the login flow took 1,585.0 ms at p50, against 1,968.8 ms with it and 1,577.6 ms published.
  - Everywhere else the read-back waited about as long without the hold, for a read that showed the text: the Compose window's re-read after the action, about 220 ms, and on the Simulator the paste or, in most fills, the keys, 247 to 297 ms. The exception is SwiftUI's username field, where the typed text showed in 92.6 ms at the median against 326.5 ms with the hold. Over a series, the hold cost 91.7 ms at p50 on SwiftUI's login, 2.4 to 41.0 ms on the other Simulator and Compose series, and 183.3 ms (text entry) and 383.8 ms (login) on the emulator's Expo fixture.
  - Neither variant retried a fill or warned. The fixtures have no field that sets its own value again after an entry, which is what the hold guards against, so these runs show its cost and not its benefit. That benefit was seen only on Bluesky's controlled search field (task 10.23).
- **Devices.** The emulator's guest neither saturated nor rebooted: its busiest 2 s tick used 58 % of its four vCPUs, during Maestro's series. On the emulator, agent-device's daemon idled on the host through all four of mobile-agent's series of the run with baselines (task 10.27, finding 5). On the Simulator, no agent-device process was seen in any mobile-agent window.
- **10.30.** With the hold kept for typed text alone, the emulator's text entry took 43.0 ms at p50 on Expo and 234.9 ms on Compose, and its login 1,586.9 and 3,488.1 ms, within 22.1 ms of the run without the hold. Against the baselines above, the login ran 14.76 and 6.75 times faster than Maestro's and 2.89 and 1.70 times faster than agent-device's. On the Simulator typed fills still hold, and a paste that changes the field is taken no sooner, since no read shows it sooner ([10.30 rerun](#1030-rerun)).
- **10.29.** In the slower cluster of the Simulator's text entries, `fill` waits 222 to 252 ms before it looks for its field: the emptied field's caret blinked while the daemon read the screen after the settle before it, and the mirror reads again no sooner than 200 ms after a read. That is recorded as the platform's. A paste's read-back, which waited out the same interval, took 69.1 to 124.1 ms with the fix, against 248.1 to 294.8 ms, so text entry took 177 to 201 ms less at the median of each cluster and 141.6 to 185.4 ms less at p95; its p50, 376.3 ms on SwiftUI and 555.4 ms on Expo, still lands in either cluster ([10.29 rerun](#1029-rerun)).
- **10.31.** A paste on the Simulator puts its text on the pasteboard with a `SET` sent from the daemon's process to the guest's `dtpasteboardd`, 8 to 35 ms against 118 to 250 ms for `devicectl`, which stays the fallback, and lists the device's applications only after they change. Text entry took 199.2 and 387.0 ms at p50 on SwiftUI and Expo, against 376.3 and 555.4 ms after 10.29, 129 to 155 ms less at each cluster's median, and 462.8 and 434.6 ms at p95, against 621.9 and 624.6; the SwiftUI login, whose fills are typed, took 4,143.3 ms against 4,198.4 ([10.31 rerun](#1031-rerun)).

- **Flags.** The harness's defaults otherwise: each platform's two fixtures, 50 measured iterations after 3 warm-up, 1 retry. No gate's series was among these, so every gate was listed as not measured, and each run exited 0.
- **The series.** `--primitive text-entry --flow login` keeps the plan's text-entry and login series; `--primitive` is new in this branch (3c51882). Text entry is mobile-agent's alone, since the baselines run flows only. A run first checks every tool's login against its golden end state on both fixtures. Then, per fixture, it measures text entry and times login through mobile-agent, Maestro and agent-device, with the harness's hand-offs between tools.
- **Fill counts.** The harness's CLI adapter now notes each fill's reply in its sample (0ae21d1): the method, whether the fill was `retried`, how it read the field back and after how long (`readBackMs`), and its warning. The counts take every timed iteration, warm-ups included; the golden checks' fills are not recorded. A text entry has two fills: the timed one, and the one after the span that empties the field.
- **The variant without the hold.**
  - `READ_BACK_HOLD` was set to `Duration::ZERO` in `crates/daemon/src/handlers/act.rs` as a local, uncommitted change, and the workspace built with the same `cargo build --release`.
  - `mobile-agent` and `mobile-agent-daemon` were copied to a directory of their own, and the source restored before any commit; `git status` was clean and a rebuild gave back the daemon above. The copied `mobile-agent` was byte for byte the release's; the variant's daemon was `7749708aad338f863cf8c3e11546e1408bb293b7f1aa83f393acaf2c84c3b975`.
  - Its runs drove that copy with `--cli`, and the copy started the daemon beside it. Their provenance records the checkout as clean, which it was while they ran; their daemon is 0ae21d1's with that one line changed.
  - Without the hold, `held` takes a match once a read that began after the entry shows it, or once nothing has changed since the latest read began.
  - Both variants ran alone on each device, one after the other: the hold as built first, then none.
- **Windows.** The harness records no time per series. Each series' window below comes from the host recorder, by which tool's commands were running, so its edges are good to about 5 s. The load is the 1-minute average, so it also carries the minute before a window. In the runs without the hold, the recorder cut the commands' arguments short, so the text-entry and login series cannot be told apart there; their load is the run's.

### What differs from the published runs

- **Code.** The published series ran at e84d86e on the Simulator and 05a4756 on the emulator; these ran at 0ae21d1, with every change since, of which 10.23's read-back is one. Two more changes to the Simulator's text path came with 10.23. An empty `fill` now clears the field with Command-A and the delete key (7d4e402); before, it left the text in the field, and the next fill's Command-A and paste replaced it. And direct text is pasted (54ee687), a method these series do not ask for.
- **The emulator's shim.** 10.1 drove mobile-agent through a shim with one more exec per command, 3.2 to 3.5 ms at p50 (its finding 9), and stopgaps for UI automation. These runs drive `target/release/mobile-agent` itself, as task 10.10 made possible.
- **Hand-offs.** In 10.1 no tool let go of the emulator: agent-device's daemon and its test input method stayed from its first golden check to the end of the run (10.1, findings 4 and 5). Here the harness releases each tool and selects the stock keyboard again after agent-device. On the Simulator agent-device's daemon, runner and bridge are stopped at the release (task 10.18); on the emulator its daemon still idles on the host for 5 minutes after `close` (finding 5).
- **Context.** The published series ran inside full runs among every primitive and flow; these ran alone, right after the golden checks of login. The published windows' 1-minute loads were 2.98 to 9.54 (median 5.53) for SwiftUI's primitives and login and 5.13 to 17.37 (median 10.39) for Expo's (10.2, Load), and 1.5 to 7.9 (median 3.7) over 10.1's run 1.

## Findings

1. **The read-back's cost depends on how soon a read shows the text, not on the hold alone.**
   - Per fill at the median it was 201 ms on the emulator's Expo fixture, 219 to 230 ms on Compose, and 263 to 337 ms on the Simulator.
   - Only on the emulator's Expo fixture, and for typed text in SwiftUI's username field, does the hold set it; the rest is the time until the field's new text shows in a read.
   - Text entry rose by 200 to 214 ms on the emulator and 296 to 490 ms on the Simulator, and login by 153 to 392 ms. The task's estimate of 0.55 s per login held for the read-backs' sum on the Simulator (546.0 and 619.2 ms); each Simulator flow grew by less.
2. **On the Simulator, text entry before its read-back comes in two clusters about 250 ms apart.**
   - 135 of 300 measured entries over three runs took 426 to 559 ms before the read-back started, against 172 to 289 ms for the rest and 179 to 301 ms in the published run.
   - The run without the hold has both clusters.
   - The series' p50 jumps between 515 and 718 ms with the clusters' shares, so one run's p50 for this series is not a stable figure; p95, 753 to 809 ms, is always in the slower cluster.
   - The one change in the series' work since the published run is that each timed fill now pastes into an empty field (7d4e402); whether that, or something in the tap, the focus wait or the paste, makes the second cluster was not tested.
   - Task 10.29 found the cause: the emptied field's caret blinks, and when it changed a frame during the read the daemon makes after the settle, `fill` waited for the next read, 222 to 252 ms ([10.29 rerun](#1029-rerun)).
3. **agent-device's login varies in ways that move its factors.**
   - On the Simulator's Expo fixture its golden check passed this time, where 10.2's failed both tries. Then 45 of its 50 iterations failed and 1 was flaky, all 95 failed attempts, warm-ups included, with `Batch failed at step 2 (fill): Selector did not match: id="login-username"`, the error of 10.2's finding 3. Its factor there, 2.14, rests on 5 iterations.
   - On the emulator's Expo fixture its successful logins fall in two clusters in both runs: 3.06 to 3.43 s and 4.54 to 4.77 s. Its median was in the first in 10.1 (20 of 34) and in the second here (23 of 38), which moved its p50 by 1.40 s and mobile-agent's factor from 2.02 to 2.33. Its 31 failed attempts all stopped at the first fill with `Android fill verification failed`, as 10.1's 44 did.
4. **Maestro's login on the emulator's Compose fixture took 3.35 s longer than published**: 23,530.6 against 20,177.4 ms at p50 (+16.6 %), and on Expo 548.0 ms longer (+2.4 %).
   - In 10.1, agent-device's test input method was the device's keyboard from the golden checks on (10.1, finding 5). Here the harness selects the stock keyboard again after each agent-device release, and the stock keyboard used 33 to 35 CPU-s in the guest per Maestro series.
   - Whether the keyboard explains the difference was not tested.
   - mobile-agent's factor against Maestro on Compose rose from 5.99 to 6.60 because of it.
5. **On the emulator, agent-device's daemon still outlives its hand-off by 5 minutes (task 10.27).** Both of mobile-agent's fixtures' series ran while an idle agent-device daemon was alive on the host, at 0.0 % CPU in all but one sample (6.1 %), holding nothing on the device. On the Simulator the release stopped everything (task 10.18).

### Why the slower cluster

- **It is the catch-up.** Each phase of the entry took as long in both clusters. The slower entries waited in the catch-up that brings the replica up to the display before `fill` looks for its field: a frame had changed after the latest read began, so it waited for the read that change calls for, which the mirror makes no sooner than `DEFAULT_MIN_REREAD_INTERVAL` (200 ms) after the latest read ends, and for that read to end. In the faster entries no frame had changed since.
- **The frames are the caret's.** With a text field focused, iOS 26.5 fades the caret out and in about once a second: four frame changes over about 110 ms for each fade, the fades 237 and 537 ms apart. Over 3 s on the SwiftUI fixture the trace counted 32 frame changes with the field focused and empty, 24 with text in it, 2 with its text selected, and 8, all within 360 ms of the settle that followed the launch, with no field focused.
- **The series meets them right after a read.** The emptying `fill` leaves the field focused and empty, so its caret blinks through the `wait --settle` that follows. The settle ends 200 ms after the last change, in a gap between fades, often the 237 ms one, which leaves about 37 ms before the next fade. The daemon's worker then reads the screen once more, frames having changed since its last read (design D5), and the timed `fill` arrived while that read ran in 77 of the 80 entries. In every slower entry a frame changed after that read had begun and before the catch-up looked, the next fade beginning 0 to 51 ms after the settle ended; in the faster ones none did.
- **Why the published run had one cluster.** Before task 10.23 the emptying `fill` pressed Command-A and typed nothing (7d4e402): the field kept its text, selected, and a selection shows no caret.
- **It is recorded as the platform's.** The blink is UIKit's, in any focused field, and the 200 ms is design D5's bound on re-reads after frames change, which any command meets after any frame change, text entry or not. A command that arrives within 200 ms of the end of a read during which the caret blinked waits for the next read; one that arrives later, as an agent's commands seconds apart do, finds the latest read over long enough that the read the blink calls for starts at once. Removing the wait would take either a read at once whenever a command needs the tree after frames changed, a shorter interval for every window, which design D5 and its lean core rule out, or telling a caret's frames from the screen's content, which a hash of the whole thumbnail cannot.

### The read-back

- **What held it back.** The read the mirror makes right after the paste never showed the text: it ended 25.2 to 57.5 ms after Command-V, and the paste's first frame change came 42.7 to 92.6 ms after it. The next read of the field's window, which sends no change events, came 200 ms after that read ended, so before the fix the read-back took the text 259.5 to 293.4 ms after the entry.
- **The fix.** After text the platform takes in one piece for a field of a window that sends no change events, pasted on a Simulator or set directly on Android, the mirror reads that window again as soon as a frame changes after its latest read began, and on a target without frames as soon as that read ends, until a read shows the field's text changed, the next input, or a poll interval after the text (`Mirror::act`; design D5; `crates/mirror/README.md`). Other windows and other input keep the interval. The mirror's tests on its fake target failed first: the field's window was read again at 730 ms, the minimum interval after the read that followed the text, instead of at 560 ms with the frame that showed the text, and without frames at the poll, 1.5 s, instead of as each read ended; the daemon's test of a fill whose fake field shows its text 60 ms after the entry took it at 203.3 ms.
- **With it.** In the trace that read began 1.3 to 1.8 ms after the paste's first frame change in 34 of 40 entries, and at once after the first read in the 6 where that frame came during it; it showed the text every time, and the read-back took 67.6 to 122.3 ms.
- **Only the field's text ends it.** Text for a Simulator field taps the field first, and on the first fill after a launch the read after the paste showed the field focused, not yet its text; the fix as first written took any change of the field as the end, and that fill took 313.0 ms. The mirror's test of a field that shows its focus before its text failed first, the field's window read at 750 ms instead of 580 ms.
- **Not after keys.** The fix as first written also re-read after text typed as keys. In a series with that build, not kept, the emptying `fill`, Command-A and the delete key, read back in 307.7 and 288.7 ms at p50 (SwiftUI, Expo), against 279.1 and 271.1 ms in the run alone above: `fill` holds typed text 200 ms, which a read at the first frame cannot end, and that read put the next one 200 ms after it. Text typed as keys keeps the interval, the mirror's test failing first with reads at 560 and 790 ms instead of 730 ms.
