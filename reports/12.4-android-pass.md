# 12.4: The third pass's Android failures replayed through the server

Task 3.1 of `android-pass-fixes` asks for a replay, through the MCP server, of the cases from the third demo pass that the change's fixes were to settle: on the emulator, Chrome's `open`, which failed there with `adb_failed` and a would-block error, five times cold with and without a `timeout`, and a cold `open` of Settings and of Clock with its first tree's time; on the Simulator, a click by coordinates on Safari's address bar, which named nothing in the pass, a click on a button with a ref, and Maps' fill by the ref its snapshot gave, which acts on a twin; then one fresh `claude -p` run of the pass's Chrome task, counted against the pass's; the replies recorded in this report, scrubbed, and the transcripts kept privately.

This report records that replay of 2026-10-24, from 07:22 to 08:10 UTC: the setup, each case with its replies beside the pass's, the counted run, the emulator's launch times over its first twenty minutes after a cold boot, which the counted run called for, the findings and what was not measured. Times are UTC. The pass is the demo pass of 2026-10-23 on main at 1cf1c4e8, which the change's `proposal.md` calls the third demo pass and `README.md` counts as the fourth pass on real apps, after those of 2026-10-20, 10-21 and 10-22: the nine tasks on the Simulator's and the emulator's own apps through the server, from which the change came; its notes, transcripts and videos are kept privately, as this replay's transcripts are, and its replies below come from its transcripts. The build is the change's at ff3cc251, which holds tasks 1.1 to 2.2. A reply is quoted as the server's text gave it, with the Simulator's identifier written `<the Simulator>` and a screenshot's directory `<dir>`.

## Verdict

Every case answered as the change designed, in every run, and none of the scripted exchanges' calls failed but the six `open`s given 300 ms, which failed as they were meant to. The counted run met a launch that took the emulator 18.9 s: its first `open` failed at the default deadline, naming the launch, and the model's second, with a timeout of 40 s, opened Chrome.

### Method

- **The exchanges.** A client of this replay's own, in Python, started the server for each exchange and spoke to it over its standard input and output, as 12.3's replay did: `initialize` at the protocol revision 2025-06-18 and `notifications/initialized`, then each call as a `tools/call` line once the call before it had its answer. It recorded every message with its time and each answer's text as it landed; a call's time below is the client's clock from the request to its answer. Each exchange is a fresh server, so a fresh session; the first call on a socket directory without a daemon started one, which served the exchanges after it: on the emulator one for the 2 GB boot's cases and the counted run, until its `shutdown` before the last launches at 2 GB, and one for the 4 GB boot's, and on the Simulator one for its six exchanges.
- **The calls** are the pass's model's where its transcripts hold them: `open {"app": "com.android.chrome", "device": "emulator-5554"}`, with `timeout` 30000 or 300 in the rounds that give one; the fill of Maps' search box by the ref of the listing right after `open`, with Lisbon. Safari's are this replay's: the address bar's box by `get box #TabBarItemTitle`, a tap at its centre, then the Close button beside the focused field by its ref, the bar again, and Close at its centre.
- **Cold.** Before each cold `open`: `adb shell am force-stop <app>`, the Home key by `adb shell input keyevent 3`, and 2 s; the Simulator's app terminated through `simctl` before each exchange.
- **The launch alone.** `adb shell am start -W -n <activity>` after the same three steps, the daemon attached, read for its `TotalTime`.
- **Runs.** Chrome in two exchanges of twelve rounds at 2 GB and one at 4 GB, each five rounds with no timeout, five with 30 s and two with 300 ms, then three launches alone; Settings and Clock three times each in one exchange per boot, then two launches alone each; Safari and Maps three exchanges each.
- **The counted run**, the pass's command: `claude -p "<task>" --mcp-config mcp.json --strict-mcp-config --allowedTools "mcp__mobile-agent__*" --max-turns 30 --output-format stream-json --verbose < /dev/null`, from the emulator's socket directory, with `mcp.json` holding the server's entry and the environment above. The task is the pass's Chrome task word for word, recovered from the client's log of that run: "Use only the mobile-agent MCP server's tools. On the Android emulator, open Chrome (com.android.chrome), get past any first-run screens without signing in, open the page example.com by typing its address in the address bar, and read the page's main heading or first sentence. Finally, report the outcome in one or two lines, and which tool calls failed, if any." Chrome was force-stopped and the home screen put in front at 07:26, and the daemon was running, as in the pass; Chrome's first-run screens had been passed in the first pass on real apps, so its cold start showed its New Tab Page.
- **Counts**, as 12.2 and 12.3 took them and through the same summary for the passes' Chrome runs: a turn is Claude Code's `num_turns`; server calls are the stream's `tool_use` blocks for the server's tools, Claude Code's own `ToolSearch` counted apart; a failed call is a `tool_result` with `is_error`; tokens are the result line's `usage`; cost is `total_cost_usd`, at the model's list price; time is the client's `duration_ms`, and a call's time the stream's, from its `tool_use` to its `tool_result`.
- **The launches over time**, after the counted run: Chrome launched three times, Settings twice and Clock twice by `am start -W` alone, each cold, with the guest's `free -m`, at set minutes after the boot; at 2 GB the last of them after `shutdown` of the daemon and 30 s; then the boot with `-memory 4096`, Chrome's, Settings' and Clock's exchanges again, and the same launches at 6.5, 10.5, 13.6 and 20 minutes. The guest's `logcat`, `dumpsys cpuinfo` and `dumpsys meminfo` were read at the slow points.
- **Load.** Each exchange and the counted run started once the 1-minute load average had been under 6, polled every 60 s: 2.5 to 6.2 through the emulator's cases at 2 GB, 1.8 to 3.1 at 4 GB, 4.8 at the counted run's start, and 5.0 to 7.0 through the Simulator's cases, whose boot took it to 114 for five minutes.

## Findings

### 1. The emulator slows down a few minutes after a cold boot, at either memory

On both boots, Chrome's cold launch by `am start -W` alone took 0.24 to 0.72 s in the first four minutes and 2.3 to 7.8 s from six minutes to twenty, and Settings' 0.21 to 0.26 s and then 2.7 to 5.7 s, at the AVD's 2 GB and with `-memory 4096` alike and with the daemon shut down; the counted run's launch took 18.9 s by Android's own count 6.5 minutes after the 2 GB boot. So the pass's opens of 10 to 19 s and its `am start -W` alone of 6.3 to 6.9 s fit this state of the emulator, and not the daemon's; how long after its boot the pass's runs came, its notes do not say to the minute. The replay's cases on the emulator came in the first five minutes after each boot, where its numbers are task 2.2's and the README's; task 2.2's runs did not record their minutes after the boot. What in the guest slows its launches was not found: its memory fills with apps Google's image starts in the background at either size, and `system_server` and the display's composition take the CPU while it is slow. A model on such an emulator meets launches past `open`'s default 10 s; the reply's advice, a longer timeout, took the counted run's model to an `open` that worked. Not a gap of the tool's: `open`'s default is the waiting commands' 10 s by design (D1, As built), and the README says what a cold launch past it needs.

### 2. A timeout given to `open` for its launch also bounds its settle

The counted run's second `open`, given 40 s for a launch that Android counted 18.9 s from the first `open`'s `START`, answered 16.75 s after it was sent, 11.7 s of them after Android counted Chrome displayed, while Chrome drew frames of 1.5 to 17.9 s, the last of them ending at about 07:27:49. With an explicit timeout, `open` waits for the app's first tree and then for the screen to settle within that timeout, where without one the settle waits at most 3 s (`crates/daemon/src/handlers/mod.rs`, `settle_launch`); the plain server's text gives no `firstTreeMs`, so the first tree's share and the settle's are not separated. The reply came with Chrome's screen still, as a settle's should; not counted as a gap.

### 3. Safari shows "Reader Available" in the address bar's place after it opens

In two of Safari's three runs the full listing right after `open` held `text "Reader Available"` where the bar's title stands, and `get box #TabBarItemTitle` waited 1.95 and 1.66 s for the title to come back, as a selector waits for a match; in the first run the title was there at once. Safari's; not a gap.

### 4. Chrome's first tree beyond `am start -W`

At 2 GB, Chrome's cold `firstTreeMs` came 0.26 to 0.80 s after the `TotalTime` of `am start -W` alone, where the README gives about 0.1 to 0.5 s beyond the launch; task 2.2 took that share beyond the launch inside `open`, which holds the `resolve-activity` lookup and adb's round trips, 709 to 1,139 ms for Chrome's against `TotalTime`'s 662 to 718 (D4), and this replay did not time the launch inside `open`. At 4 GB the share was 0.08 to 0.77 s. Within what the README says once the launch inside `open` is counted; not a gap.

## What was not measured

- **What slows the emulator.** Its cause in the guest or in the emulator, whether it passes after twenty minutes, whether a system image without Google's apps slows the same way, and whether the host's handling of the emulator's window, which nobody looked at during the runs, plays a part.
- **The first tree's and the settle's shares of the counted run's second `open`**, the server serving the plain text the pass's client saw.
- **Safari's `click #TabBarItemTitle` by its identifier**, the pass's own call, whose hit, a plain text that no listing gives a ref, takes `(no ref)` by the same rule (D2, As built).
- **The pass's other Android gaps**, which the change leaves as limitations: Clock's minute field under the time picker's overlay without a ref, a `wait` with text matching Chrome's omnibox suggestion, and the click's 200 ms window on Android, which the counted run's click on the search box met and the card's change wait collected.
- **Variance.** Two boots; three exchanges of Chrome's cold `open`, one of Settings' and Clock's per boot, three of Safari's and Maps'; one counted run, whose counts are one sample.
- **The first pass's model on this build**, `claude-opus-5-5`, and Codex.
