# 11.1: physical iPhone run

Tasks 7.1 to 7.3 of `physical-ios` ask for the harness on a physical iPhone: every primitive and the three reference flows through `mobile-agent`, 50 measured iterations after 3 warm-up, judged against the `ios-device` gates (7.1); the same flows through the baselines task 6.3 brought up on the phone, with the hand-offs between them (7.2); and what the device changes for the mirror, from the raw data (7.3).

This report records those runs on 2026-10-10 on an iPhone SE (3rd generation) with iOS 26.6.2. Times are UTC. The harness's reports are under `reports/11.1-ios-device/`, one directory per phase (`a-swiftui-primitives/` to `n-expo-big-screen-baselines/`, with the reruns beside them), and the probes of 7.3 under `probes/`. A number below cites the file it comes from by its path under that directory.

Task 9.6 measured again, on 2026-10-11 with the daemon group 9 fixed (tasks 9.1 to 9.5, and 9.7 and 9.8 for the phases from `a5` on), what the gaps had disturbed: every primitive of both fixtures, Expo's list-scroll, and the three flows of both fixtures beside the baselines, each phase in a directory named after the 7.x phase it repeats with `-fixed` (`a6-swiftui-primitives-fixed/`, `e2-expo-primitives-fixed/`, `g2-expo-list-scroll-fixed/`, `i2-swiftui-login-baselines-fixed/` to `n-expo-big-screen-baselines-fixed/`, with their reruns), and the probes of 7.3 again (`probes/*-fixed.*`). The tables keep the 7.x numbers and add the fixed run's beside them, marked "fixed". The fixed run met a phone whose developer services failed at times, which finding 11 records.

## Verdict

- **The fixed run (9.6, 2026-10-11)**, with group 9's fixes in the daemon and the harness and a quiet host before each phase:
  - **Gates.** All four pass on both fixtures, as in 7.x (`a6-swiftui-primitives-fixed/`, `e2-expo-primitives-fixed/`, `e3-expo-primitives-fixed/`): CLI start 7.0 and 7.6 ms, cold attach 4,052.0 and 2,301.3 ms, warm snapshot 8.6 and 9.4 ms, every one served from the replica, tap dispatch 55.1 and 54.6 ms, and tap to frame change reported at 364.0 and 179.7 ms.
  - **The comparison.** `mobile-agent` kept the phone through every flow beside the baselines and passed every iteration: at p50 login 6,289.6 and 5,832.1 ms, list-scroll 30,584.7 and 28,377.2 ms (25,749.9 alone on Expo), big-screen 4,607.4 and 3,397.8 ms on SwiftUI and Expo, 0.93 to 0.98 times its 7.x time but on Expo's list-scroll beside the baselines. WebDriverAgent was faster on every flow but Expo's big-screen, `mobile-agent` taking 1.06 and 1.03 times its time on login, 2.77 and 2.73 times on list-scroll and 1.31 times on SwiftUI's big-screen, and 0.89 times on Expo's; agent-device took 1.80 and 1.85 times `mobile-agent`'s on login, 0.79 and 1.14 times on big-screen, and its list-scroll failed its golden check as before (The flows on the fixed run).
  - **Findings 1 to 5 and 7.** The gate table shows what each snapshot cost the daemon (1); a still screen is polled every 10 s, so 3.0% of idle snapshots met a read against 60% and 47%, and the screenshot primitive met one in 1 of 50 iterations against 27 and 18 (2); a command 2.5 s after the last no longer lists the phone, 15.6 to 27.9 ms against 182 to 226 (3); no command waited on a daemon timeout, though the host's launch holds still reach a run through each command's own process and a new daemon's first listing (4); no runner connection was lost, and `mobile-agent`'s flows beside the baselines passed every iteration where 7.x's failed 86 to 100 % after a loss (5); and the alert's message is listed on both fixtures (7).
  - **What it moved.** The first snapshot after a relaunch now waits for a read the target's worker begins as `open` returns, about 670 ms on SwiftUI, where only the frames changed, and 450 ms on Expo, where React Native adds cells: 766.1 and 457.8 ms at p50 against 177.0 and 558.8, while `open` returns about a second sooner on SwiftUI. An idle worker still reads about 5% of the time, a read of 450 to 650 ms each 10 s by the bound's construction (Read costs, The idle poll).
  - **The phone.** Its developer services failed twice that day: in the morning DVT's screenshot answered nothing and the runner stalled on the 43rd emptying set of every session, until a reboot; in the afternoon DVT failed again and came back by itself. Tasks 9.7 and 9.8 were written from the first, and their containment ran on the phone during the second (finding 11).
- **Gates** (7.1, 50 measured iterations after 3 warm-up, each at p95; `a-swiftui-primitives/`, `e-expo-primitives/`). All four pass on both fixtures:
  - CLI start, 10.0 and 5.0 ms against 20 ms;
  - cold attach, the runner installed, 2,603.1 ms (SwiftUI) and 2,804.1 ms (Expo) against 5,000 ms;
  - warm snapshot of the 500-cell Big tab, 13.7 and 11.1 ms against 1,000 ms;
  - tap dispatch through the host's `dtuhidd`, 55.4 and 54.9 ms against 150 ms;
  - tap to frame change, reported with no verdict: 360.1 and 241.6 ms.
  - The warm snapshot passes on the daemon's replica, as on the Simulator. The runner's read of the same screens, 450 to 650 ms, is what the 1 s limit was set from, and no warm snapshot waits for it (finding 1).
- **The comparison.** On the phone, `mobile-agent`'s flows took 1.3 to 2.0 times the Simulator's at p50: login 6,662.1 and 6,262.7 ms, list-scroll 32,100.5 and 27,462.0 ms, big-screen 4,690.0 and 3,658.4 ms on SwiftUI and Expo. WebDriverAgent was faster than `mobile-agent` on every flow but Expo's big-screen: 1.13 and 1.10 times on login, 2.92 and 2.66 times on list-scroll, whose 9 swipes beat 22 to 25 resting strokes that each settle and read, and 1.35 times on SwiftUI's big-screen over the 6 iterations its session lasted. agent-device was slower than `mobile-agent` on login, by 1.70 and 1.73 times, and faster on SwiftUI's big-screen, by 1.34 times; its list-scroll failed its golden check on both fixtures, as task 6.3 found. On Expo's big-screen, run beside the baselines on the third try, `mobile-agent` was the fastest: 3,385.2 ms at p50, against 3,744.1 for WebDriverAgent over the 23 iterations its session lasted and 3,940.9 for agent-device. On the Simulator `mobile-agent` had been 2.27 and 1.24 times faster than agent-device on SwiftUI's login and big-screen (`reports/10.2-simulator.md`). `mobile-agent`'s own flows beside the baselines lost the runner's connection in every phase from `i` to `m` and failed 86 to 100 % of their iterations, so its numbers here are 7.1's, run alone (finding 5); only Expo's big-screen, on its third try, kept the connection and passed 50 of 50.
- **What the device changes for the mirror** (7.3, What the device changes for the mirror):
  - a read of the 500-cell tab costs 450 to 650 ms, against 150 ms on the Simulator; the warm snapshot never pays it, and the first snapshot after a relaunch pays the device listing (177.0 ms on SwiftUI) or the listing and the tail of a read (558.8 ms on Expo) rather than a read (findings 1 and 3);
  - a tap's first changed frame comes 340.0 and 219.6 ms after the dispatch at p50, 4.7 and 4.6 times the Simulator's, and its first tree delta 592.6 and 347.1 ms (finding 8);
  - settle ended on frames on SwiftUI in 50 of 50 and on the tree on Expo in 44 of 50, as on the Simulator, in 350.4 and 185.3 ms at p50;
  - the idle poll keeps the target's worker reading about half the time, so a command waits up to 733 ms behind it, as every other SwiftUI screenshot did (finding 2);
  - of task 6.1's three tree-read findings, `row-12` is the row the SE's floating tab bar covers (finding 6), the alert's message is missing from both platforms' listings (finding 7), and the warm snapshot is served from the replica (finding 1).
- **The host and the phone.** Other sessions' builds kept the 1-minute load past 4 for minutes in most phases, and the host held process launches for up to 130.6 s (findings 4 and 10). The runner's connection was lost in six phases (`h2`, `i` to `m`) and in a probe, after which the daemon kept the target disconnected until it stopped (finding 5); WebDriverAgent lost its session the same way in three phases, and agent-device recovered. After the harness installed the Expo build again at 12:29, the phone refused to launch it ("Unable to Verify App: An internet connection is required to verify trust of the developer") until it had verified the build; Expo's big-screen beside the baselines ran on its third try at 13:50 (`n3`), with the load past 4 for most of it and WebDriverAgent's session lost halfway (Baselines).

### Method

```sh

  --auto-lock-off --fixture swiftui --ios-app swiftui=fixtures/swiftui/build/Build/Products/Debug-iphoneos/FixtureSwiftUI.app \
  --no-baselines --primitive cli-start --primitive cold-attach … --primitive settle --out <dir>      # a-swiftui-primitives
# the same with --flow login, --flow list-scroll or --flow big-screen in place of the primitives (b, c, d),
# with --fixture expo --ios-app expo=fixtures/expo/build/device/build/Build/Products/Release-iphoneos/FixtureExpo.app (e to h),
# and without --no-baselines for the flows beside the baselines (i to n)
```

- **Counts.** 50 measured iterations after 3 warm-up and 1 retry everywhere, the baselines included: the longest phase with the baselines, `i` at 25 min 42 s, stayed under the 2 hours past which the plan allowed 20 after 2.

### 2. The idle poll keeps the target's worker reading about half the time

- **Measured.** With nothing driving the phone, the worker was reading when 60% of the probe's SwiftUI snapshots arrived and 47% of its Expo ones; those waited 160 to 733 ms (`probes/idle-swiftui.jsonl`, `probes/idle-expo.jsonl`). - **Why.** The mirror polls a window without events 1 s after its last read began (`DEFAULT_POLL_INTERVAL`), and a read of the 500-cell tab takes 450 to 650 ms on the phone, against 150 ms on the Simulator, so the poll that costs the Simulator 15% of the worker's time costs the phone 45 to 65%, and every command meets it at random.
- **Follow-up.** design.md's open question on the idle poll (task 5.2) has its numbers: a poll interval that grows with the read's cost, or no poll while the frames show no change, would give that time back; the frames already tell a still screen.
- **Outcome after the fix (9.6).** Task 9.1 polls a still screen of a slow-frames target every 10 s and re-reads it when its frames change. On the fixed run 3.0% of the idle probe's snapshots met a read on each fixture, against 60% and 47%, the reads 9.78 to 10.23 s apart, and the screenshot primitive met one in 1 of 50 iterations on each fixture, 207.7 / 227.3 and 139.8 / 202.2 ms at p50 / p95 against 843.6 / 968.1 and 214.9 / 776.2 (`probes/idle-*-fixed.jsonl`, `a6-swiftui-primitives-fixed/report.json`, `e2-expo-primitives-fixed/report.json`). The worker still reads about 5% of the time while idle, a read of 450 to 650 ms each 10 s, by the bound's construction: 5.2% and 4.2% at least in the probe (The idle poll).

### 3. Every command after 2 s without one lists the devices again, 150 to 220 ms on an iPhone

- **Measured.** A snapshot 2.5 s after the last command waited 182.2 to 226.0 ms on SwiftUI with nothing read (`probes/reads-swiftui.jsonl`); one within 2 s, 6.0 to 10.0 ms. The first snapshot after a relaunch, whose `open` took 2.5 to 3.7 s, waited 146.5 to 153.5 ms.
- **Why.** `Registry::resolve` asks the drivers again when its listing is 2 s old (`DISCOVERY_TTL`), and the iOS driver's listing probes lockdown and runs `security find-identity` and `security find-certificate` for the signing identity: 162.7 and 177.2 ms at p50 and p95 with the SE in task 5.1, 45 ms of it `security`.
- **Follow-up.** A target that is attached needs no readiness probe to be resolved, and the signing identity does not change between commands: caching the identity check, or resolving an attached target without listing, would take this off every agent's first command after a pause.
- **Outcome after the fix (9.6).** Task 9.2 resolves an attached target without listing and keeps the identity check: a snapshot 2.5 s after the last command took 19.9 to 23.5 ms on SwiftUI and 15.6 to 27.9 ms on Expo, against 182.2 to 226.0 ms (`probes/reads-swiftui-fixed.jsonl`, `probes/reads-expo-fixed.jsonl`).

### 4. Launch stalls on the host reach the daemon's commands through that listing

- **Measured.** Twice in the primitives and flows alone a command got no answer from the daemon within 30 s (`daemon_timeout`): a `wait --settle` in SwiftUI's tap-dispatch series and a `click` in SwiftUI's login, whose retry then took 30.2 s (`a-swiftui-primitives/report.json`, `b-swiftui-login/report.json`), and once more in Expo's big-screen rerun, 37.5 s (`h3-expo-big-screen/report.json`). Three Expo list-scroll iterations took 56.6 to 73.7 s with the same 22 strokes as the others' 26.1 to 28.9 s, and set that series' p95 (`g-expo-list-scroll/report.json`). A probe timing `security find-identity -v -p codesigning` and `/usr/bin/true` every 5 s from 10:31 to 11:07, beside phases `h3` and `i`, saw 17 of its 640 launches take over a second, up to 130.6 s and 17.7 s, against medians of 44.6 and 5.6 ms (`probes/launches.txt`).
- **Why, as far as it was traced.** The host's endpoint security inspects process starts, and at times held them. The bench starts a process per command, and the iOS driver's listing (finding 3) starts two `security` processes on the daemon's request path, synchronously and with no timeout (`crates/ios/src/identity.rs`), so a held launch holds the command. Which stall met which iteration was not traced: the samples carry no times.
- **Follow-up.** The same cache as finding 3 takes the daemon off this path; a timeout on the identity check would bound it otherwise.
- **Outcome after the fix (9.6).** No command of the fixed phases got a `daemon_timeout`, and no snapshot of an attached target waited on a listing. The holds themselves stayed on the host and still reach a run through the process launches the bench makes for every command and the listing a new daemon makes once: in `a5` four cold attaches took 5.4 to 29.5 s and one failed its first try with lockdown silent for 10 s in the minutes the sampler's own launches stalled for 2 min 39 s and 3 min 15 s, against 2.0 to 2.4 s for the others (`a5-swiftui-primitives-fixed/report.json`, `loads.txt`), and in `g2` the last three iterations took 54.3, 50.9 and 33.0 s against 24.8 to 26.5 s while launches of `/usr/bin/true` took up to 15.1 s from 16:22, another session's build running (`g2-expo-list-scroll-fixed/report.json`, `loads.txt`).

### 5. After a lost runner connection the daemon keeps the target disconnected until it stops

- **Measured.** In the first Expo big-screen rerun, 27 iterations passed; then the runner's connection fell silent (`nothing arrived from the other side for 3.0 s`), one attempt found lockdown not answering within 10 s and one CLI command ran out its 120 s, and every later attempt, 49 of them over 26 iterations, failed at once with the same `disconnected` from the same daemon, so 52% of the series failed (`h2-expo-big-screen/report.json`); the phone was ready and attached again in 4.3 s six minutes later (`probes/attaches.txt`). In every flow phase beside the baselines, `i` to `m`, `mobile-agent`'s series lost the connection the same way after 1 to 10 iterations, in four of them a 30 s daemon timeout first and in `l` a 10 s wait for the welcome, and failed 86 to 100 % of its iterations, while the next phase's daemon attached at once (`i-swiftui-login-baselines/` to `m-expo-list-scroll-baselines/`). `n3`'s series, run after the phone had been left alone for an hour, kept its connection through 53 iterations (`n3-expo-big-screen-baselines/`). - **Why.** The mirror stops reading once its backend reports `disconnected` and refuses actions until the target is attached again (`crates/mirror/README.md`), and the daemon's worker attaches a target once and detaches it only when the daemon stops or a command panics (`crates/daemon/src/worker.rs`). The driver's own containment starts over on a new attach (task 3.6), which this path never reaches. The runner's restart after the loss failed, likely because the silent runner still held the phone's automation session. What silenced it was not found; the first loss fell in a window where the run's own load sampler stalled for 2 min 21 s, as it did when the host held launches (finding 10), and the probe's had no sampler running.
- **Follow-up.** The worker could detach a target whose driver reports a lost connection, so that the next command attaches again and ends the old runner first, as an attach already does with a runner left from a lost session.
- **Outcome after the fix (9.6).** Task 9.3 detaches a target whose runner connection was lost, so the next command attaches again, ending the runner left behind (checked on the phone with the runner ended twice mid-session, `probes/runner-loss-9x.jsonl`), and task 9.7 ends and starts again a runner that stops answering. On the fixed run no runner connection was lost: `mobile-agent`'s series beside the baselines passed every iteration of `i2` to `n2`, where 7.x's failed 86 to 100 % after a loss, and every frames check before and after a phase found the runner ready with no restart (each phase's `frames.txt`). The only refusals of the runner were Expo's 43rd emptying set, once in each of `e2` and `e3`, which the retry passed with no restart; the attaches that failed in `e3` and `i` were the phone's DVT refusing the connection, not a lost runner (finding 11). What silenced 7.x's runner did not come back, and WebDriverAgent's session, on the same `idevice` xctest path, did not drop either but where the phone refused it in `i`.
