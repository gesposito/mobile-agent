# 10.3: physical Android device run

Task 10.3 of `mobile-automation-foundation` asks for the harness on a physical Android device, with its tap acknowledgement and warm snapshot gates verified. Scope, as decided for the lent phone: mobile-agent through the whole harness, every flow and the gates; then Maestro and agent-device on the login flow only, for one comparison on real hardware.

This report records that run on 2026-10-07 on a Nexus 5X with Android 8.1 (API 27), the oldest level anything of the project had run on: every earlier run used the API 36 emulator. Times are UTC. The harness's reports are under `reports/10.3-physical-android/`: `agent/` for mobile-agent alone, `login/` for the login flow with the baselines, and `gates.json`, the gate file both runs took.

## Verdict

- **The agent did not start on Android 8.1.** It called three platform members that the level lacks. It ended at the first windows change, every snapshot failed with `internal`, and no eviction reached the node cache, so a Compose window would have been read again from the cache. Agent 0.4.3 (552c9e2) gates each read on its level, and the agent then served the whole run ([What Android 8.1 needed in the agent](#what-android-81-needed-in-the-agent)).
- **Gates** ([Gates](#gates)), 20 measured iterations after 2 warm-up:
  - pass: CLI start (6.3 ms at p95), warm snapshot of the 500-cell screen (Expo 7.0 ms, Compose 8.7 ms), and tap acknowledgement, the click's `dispatchMs` (Expo 16.4 ms, Compose 11.4 ms, against 50 ms);
  - fail: cold attach, against 1.5 s: Expo 13,889.4 ms at p95, with 18 of 20 samples at 2.09 to 2.48 s and 2 whose `open` did not settle within 10 s, and Compose 3,131.5 ms, every sample 2.92 to 3.39 s;
  - not measured: tap to frame change, since a device has no frame stream.
- **Flows.** Login passed every iteration on both fixtures: 2,552.3 ms at p50 on Expo and 7,450.2 ms on Compose. Expo's big-screen flow took 9,500.8 ms. The other three failed their golden checks, so they were not timed:
  - Compose's list-scroll and big-screen: on Android 8.1 the launch gives the login form's first field focus, Compose raises the keyboard for it, and the keyboard covers the tab bar the flows tap first (finding 3).
  - Expo's list-scroll: `scroll --until` stopped at the end of the list with `row-200` cut off at the bottom edge of its container (finding 5).
- **Login against the baselines** ([Login against the baselines](#login-against-the-baselines)). At p50 mobile-agent's login took 2,723.4 ms on Expo and 7,347.4 ms on Compose. That was 23.10 and 7.58 times faster than Maestro 2.10.0, against 14.76 and 6.75 on the emulator, and 3.77 times faster than agent-device 0.21.14 on Compose, against 1.70. agent-device's Expo login was not timed: after its golden check and first warm-up passed, all 42 of its later attempts failed at the first `fill` with `Selector did not match: id="login-username"`, the error 10.26 recorded on the Simulator.
- **What differed from the API 36 emulator.**
  - The phone is slower wherever the device itself works:
    - starting the agent's runtime with `app_process` alone takes about 1.1 s;
    - a tap is acknowledged in about 8 ms at p50, against 4 to 5 ms;
    - Compose's 500-cell screen reads cold in 1.5 s, against 0.35 s;
    - an app's relaunch takes 1.7 s (Expo) and 3.0 to 5.7 s (Compose).
  - Reads the replica serves are as fast as on the emulator: a warm snapshot took 5.9 to 6.2 ms at p50.
  - Android 8.1 itself brought what the emulator never showed: the three reads the agent lacked, a launch whose focus raises Compose's keyboard, and one node cache for the whole process. With that cache an eviction empties everything, and on Compose's 500-cell screen commands waited about 10 s behind the reads (finding 6, [Old-level paths](#old-level-paths)).

## What Android 8.1 needed in the agent

The first `open` (14:22) failed with `agent_failed`: "the agent ended before it was ready (exit status 0); its output: ". The agent's log had the cause:

```
D AndroidRuntime: Calling main entry dev.mobileagent.agent.Main
E AndroidRuntime: *** FATAL EXCEPTION IN SYSTEM PROCESS: a11y-callbacks
E AndroidRuntime: java.lang.NoSuchMethodError: No virtual method getWindowChanges()I in class Landroid/view/accessibility/AccessibilityEvent; or its super classes (declaration of 'android.view.accessibility.AccessibilityEvent' appears in /system/framework/framework.jar!classes2.dex)
E AndroidRuntime: 	at dev.mobileagent.agent.EventHub.payloads(SourceFile:114)
E AndroidRuntime: 	at dev.mobileagent.agent.EventHub$1.onAccessibilityEvent(SourceFile:84)
```

UI automation itself was granted: the agent took the role on the `user` build and was receiving events when it ended. `onEvent` catches `RuntimeException` only, so the `Error` ended the process.

Three members were missing:

| Read | Exists from | On Android 8.1, with agent 0.4.2 | 0.4.3 (`Levels.kt`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `AccessibilityEvent.getWindowChanges()`, for a windows change | Android 9 (API 28) | `NoSuchMethodError` on the callback thread; the agent ended at the first windows change | before Android 9 a windows change carries no detail and names no window, so it is reported as a state change, which the mirror answers with a full read as it does every window event |
| `AccessibilityAction.ACTION_PAGE_UP`, `_DOWN`, `_LEFT` and `_RIGHT`, in `Semantics`' scroll action sets | Android 10 (API 29) | `NoSuchFieldError` in a static initializer: every snapshot failed with `internal`, "snapshot failed inside the agent: java.lang.NoSuchFieldError: No static field ACTION_PAGE_UP …" | before Android 10 no node can offer a page action, so the sets hold the scroll actions alone |
| `AccessibilityInteractionClient.getCache(int)`, the connection's node cache, which eviction clears | not on Android 8.1; present on API 36 | `cache()` found no method and returned null, so `clearCache` and `evictWindow` did nothing: the Compose poll's re-read of the Login tab, 56 nodes, came back in 1 round trip, from the cache | where the method is missing, the process's one cache, the static `sAccessibilityCache`, is the connection's; `evictWindow` then clears all of it, since that cache has no public `clearSubTree` |

- **How they were found.** The jar's dex was compared with the SDK's `api-versions.xml` for every platform method, field and class it references above API 26, the dex's minimum. R8 had moved each such call into a method of its own. Of the 11 references, 6 were already gated on their level: `UiAutomation.clearCache` (API 34), `getBoundsInWindow` (34), the prefetch overloads `getChild(int, int)` and `getRoot(int)` (33), the node copy constructor (30) and `getRegionInScreen` (30). The other 5 are the first two rows: `getWindowChanges` and the four page actions. The cache is a hidden member that no such table lists; a reflection probe run with `app_process` on the phone listed the client's `sAccessibilityCache` field and public `clearCache()`, and `AccessibilityCache`'s public `clear()` and private `clearSubTreeLocked(int, long)`, with no `getCache(int)` and no `clearSubTree(AccessibilityNodeInfo)`.
- **The cost on API 36.** None of note: the window bits and the page actions are inline comparisons of the level, once per windows change and once per process; the cache lookup is the one it always made, and the fallback runs only where that method is missing.
- **Fail-first checks.** `unit-test/LevelsTest.kt` checks the three gates on the host JVM with reads that throw what Android 8.1 threw and with client classes holding their cache as each level does. With `Levels.kt` still reading as 0.4.2 did, `codec-test.sh --host-only` failed the three older-level checks with `NoSuchMethodError`, `NoSuchFieldError` and `NoSuchMethodException`, and passed the four others; with the gates, 7 of 7. On the phone, with agent 0.4.2, the host tests `handshake`, `tree` and `events` passed 2 of 23. The others failed with "the agent printed no ready line", or, where the agent came up, with a snapshot answered `internal`. With 0.4.3 all 11 of `handshake` passed, and so did the two snapshot tests of `tree` rerun on the final jar that had failed with the `NoSuchFieldError`.
- **The host tests that still fail on the phone** encode the emulator. `fixture::launch` waits 800 ms after `am start -W`, where Expo's Big screen took 5 to 8 s to list its cells here (first snapshot, below); on Android 8.1 the Compose fixture's launch raises the keyboard (finding 3), so an `input tap` on a tab lands on the keyboard; `a_keyboard_appearing_is_reported_on_the_window_stream` waits for an `added` window change, a kind Android 8.1 cannot report; and `editing_one_react_native_field_emits_events_naming_that_field_only` allows the read after an edit the window count plus 6 round trips, where the phone took 15 with a build gating the first two reads alone and 38 with 0.4.3, whose evictions clear the shared cache. Making them level-aware is left for later; the run below exercised the same paths through the daemon.

## Old-level paths

The agent has a path of its own for several things below Android 11 to 14 (`agent-android/README.md`, Serving the protocol). What each did on this phone:

1. **The keyboard window's bounds.** Before Android 11 there is no `getRegionInScreen`, so the input-method window keeps the bounds the platform gives it (task 10.25's `KeyboardArea` does nothing).
   - Gboard sat docked at the bottom on the Compose login form. Its window's frame is (0, 63) to (1080, 1920) pixels (`dumpsys window`), and its content inset puts the keyboard's top at 1,053 pixels; its touchable insets are a region.
   - `click id=login-submit` tapped (181, 705) pixels, 348 pixels above the keyboard, and replied with no "0% of it in view" warning. `wait --text=Welcome` found `login-status` at 809 to 872 pixels. The tab bar at the bottom of the app window, under the keyboard, was cut from `snapshot -i`, and a screenshot shows the keyboard over it.
   - So on Android 8.1 the platform's bounds for this keyboard are those of what it covers, and nothing above it counted as hidden, unlike the whole screen on the Simulator (task 10.21) and the box around Gboard's floating toolbar and navigation strip on the API 36 emulator (task 10.25). This Gboard showed no floating toolbar, so a region in pieces far apart did not come up.
2. **The input path.** Before API 34 the agent injects through `InputManager.injectInputEvent` with `WAIT_FOR_RESULT`, as `InputManagerGlobal` does not exist. Tap dispatch, the daemon's span from the request to the agent's acceptance of the down and up events, took 8.1 / 16.4 / 22.0 ms (p50 / p95 / max) on Expo and 8.6 / 11.4 / 11.5 ms on Compose, against 4.1 / 4.8 / 5.3 and 5.1 / 6.7 / 8.6 ms on the emulator's agent path. That is about twice the emulator's at p50 and passes the 50 ms bound by a factor of 3 at p95.
3. **The cold read without the prefetch API.** Before API 33 the walk reads each child with the platform's own prefetch, without the breadth-first overloads. The host test of the Compose 500-cell screen traced its first read at 1,471 ms (1,685 ms in an earlier run) over 468 round trips for 591 nodes, and its warm reads at 61 to 126 ms over 3. Spike S1 counted 465 round trips and 349 ms on the API 36 emulator. The round trips are the same, about one per cell past the 49th, since a batch holds at most 50 nodes either way. Each one costs 3.1 to 3.6 ms on this phone against 0.75 ms. The harness's Compose first snapshot hides most of it, since `open --relaunch` has waited for the first tree: 121.5 ms at p50.
4. **Frames.** `devices` lists the phone as `android-device` and says nothing of frames. `doctor` has no frames line and skips `emulator-control` with "no emulator is running". `status` says `frames: none; screenshots come from the agent on demand` with `frameSource: false`, and the bench's provenance records no frame source. `screenshot` replied with `source` `capture` and `ageMs` 0, a 562x1000 JPEG from `UiAutomation.takeScreenshot`, in 100.0 ms at p50 on Expo's Big screen (finding 6 for Compose's).
5. **`fill` with the read-back.** Every fill was `direct`, and none of the 176 fills of the run alone, nor the 88 of the run with the baselines, was retried or warned. `readBackMs` at p50 was 57.3 to 57.7 ms on Expo, whose fields report a change by event, against 21 ms on the emulator, and 386.4 to 525.3 ms on Compose, whose window is read again after the action, against 218 to 228 ms. On Android 8.1 that re-read is a cold read, since the eviction before it clears the whole cache (What Android 8.1 needed in the agent).
6. **The overlay rule of task 10.28.** No fixture screen has a view drawn over a sibling: the System tab's in-app alert is a window of its own. The rule's `on_top` flag rests on `getDrawingOrder`, which exists from API 24, and it was not exercised here.
7. **The rest.** The window-size check of a `transformed` window needs Android 14, so on 8.1 only a frame off the display marks one (`agent-android/README.md`, Serving the protocol). The 500 ms hold after a stroke that leaves a list at its end answers the stretch Android 12 draws (`DEFAULT_OVERSCROLL` in `crates/mirror/src/mirror.rs`). It is not gated on the level, so it also applies on 8.1, which draws a glow that takes no touch. Settles took 186.1 and 228.5 ms at p50, as on the emulator.

## Findings

### 1. Agent 0.4.2 could not serve Android 8.0 to 9 (fixed in 0.4.3)

[What Android 8.1 needed in the agent](#what-android-81-needed-in-the-agent) has the evidence and the fix. The dex is built with `--min-api 26`, but nothing checked its platform calls against that level. Lint's NewApi check does that in a Gradle build, and the agent's build has no Gradle, so the dex scan of this report was by hand. A check of the jar's references above API 26 against `api-versions.xml`, failing on any not gated on its level, would have caught both public members on any host, without a device. The hidden cache method is not in that table, so only a device can show such a gap.

### 2. Cold attach takes 2 to 3.4 s on this phone, over its 1.5 s gate

- **Measured.** 2,193.7 and 3,050.6 ms at p50 on Expo and Compose, every sample over 1.5 s. Two of Expo's 20 took 13.9 s, whose `open` did not settle within 10 s ([Gates](#gates)).
- **Where the time goes.**
  - Starting the runtime: `app_process` took 1.1 to 1.4 s on this phone, before any of the agent's code runs, against well under a second for the emulator's whole attach.
  - Pushing the jar, taking UI automation and waiting for the first tree come on top, and then the open's settle: 0.32 to 0.36 s on Expo, 0.50 to 0.64 s on Compose.
  - The gate's 1.5 s, set on the emulator, does not leave room for that runtime start on a device of this class.
- **Not isolated.**
  - How the agent's own start divides between its class loading, UI automation's connection and the wait for an active window. The agent's ready line reports the last two (`connect_ms`, `window_wait_ms`), and the daemon does not pass it on.
  - What kept the two Expo opens from settling.
- **Possible ways on.** Keep the agent running between daemons, as the driver already reuses an agent a previous daemon left (`crates/android/README.md`, Agent deployment). The bench's cold attach stops the daemon, and the daemon's `shutdown` stops its agents. Or restate the gate per device class.

### 3. On Android 8.1 the Compose fixture's launch raises the keyboard over the tab bar

- **What happens.** Every `open --relaunch dev.mobileagent.fixture.compose` on the Login tab ended with `login-username` focused and the keyboard up (`mInputShown=true`, in touch mode), and `snapshot -i` listed 5 refs: the form and the navigation bar's Home button. The tab bar lay under the keyboard and was cut, which a screenshot confirms. After Back hid the keyboard, the four other tabs were listed again. The Expo fixture's launch left no field focused and the keyboard down.
- **Why.** Android 8.1 gives a window's first focusable view focus at its first layout even in touch mode. Android 9 stopped doing so for apps that target it, so the API 36 emulator never showed this. Compose passes the focus to its first text field and shows the keyboard for it.
- **The effect on the harness.** The Compose list-scroll and big-screen flows tap a tab right after the launch, so their golden checks failed on both tries with `element_not_found` for `tab-list` and `tab-big`, and neither was timed. The primitives open their screens through `initial_tab` and the login flow needs no tab, so they ran. A baseline would meet the same keyboard, but only login ran through them.
- **And after the submit.** The contract says submitting dismisses the keyboard. On this phone the keyboard stayed up after `click id=login-submit`, with `mShowExplicitlyRequested=true` from the fills' focus.
- **Possible ways on**, not taken here: a fixture that asks for no initial focus, or a flow step that closes the keyboard, on every tool alike. Either changes the fixtures or the flows for every platform, and the emulator's numbers would no longer be like for like.

### 4. `scroll` strokes across the keyboard, and Gboard types the strokes into the focused field

- **What happened.** In the derisk, `scroll --until id=row-150 down 600` was sent once on the Compose Login tab, which has no row 150, with the keyboard up. It made 50 strokes of 556.6 points down, the most it takes, each over most of the 731-point screen's height, and failed with `element_not_found`. Each stroke started low in the keyboard and moved up across it, from Gboard's bottom row of letters past its top row. Gboard read each as glide typing and entered "by" into `login-username` 49 times: the field read `agent by by by …` afterwards, and a screenshot shows it.
- **Why it ran on.** The scroll ends once the content stops moving. The field's text changed with every stroke, so it never saw a still screen.
- **What it means.** It is not a matter of Android 8.1: a stroke that starts over an input-method window reaches the keyboard on any device whose keyboard is up, and with Gboard it types. A stroke could keep out of an input-method window's bounds, as the mirror already knows them for visibility, or the scroll could refuse a stroke that crosses one.

### 5. Expo's list-scroll check stopped at the end of the list with `row-200` cut off

- **The check.** The harness's golden check of Expo's list-scroll failed on both tries. Each time `scroll --until id=row-200 down 600` ended after 24 and 25 strokes of 460.6 points with `element_not_found`: "id=row-200 was on screen but not mostly in view (cut off at the bottom edge of its scroll container, which scrolls on) …: the content stopped moving". So the flow was not timed.
- **By hand, after both runs,** the same command on the same build succeeded: `open --relaunch --env initial_tab=list` on Expo, then 25 strokes of 460.6 points, `visible` 1.0, `row-200` at 579.4 to 627.4 points with the tab bar below it.
- **What it says.** The scroll's stop at the list's end is intermittent on this phone. In both tries of the check, it took the content for still while the last row still lay across the container's edge. By hand, it stopped with the row in full view. On the emulator the flow passed every iteration (`reports/10.14-agent-path/report.md`, 13.6 s at p50).
- **Not isolated.** Whether React Native's list on this slower phone reports its last scroll position late, or the settle after the last stroke ends too early on a level without the stretch, needs a run of the scroll with the agent's trace.

### 6. Compose's screenshots took 5 to 11 s on the 500-cell screen

- **Measured.** In the run alone, Compose's 22 screenshots of the Big screen took 5,126.3 to 11,323.1 ms, and Expo's 92.9 to 196.2 ms.
- **By hand, after both runs,** through a fresh daemon:
  - On Compose's Big screen:
    - `open --relaunch --env initial_tab=big` replied after 22.6 s with `settled: false` and a first tree at 12.2 s;
    - `wait id=cell-500` took 10.0 s;
    - three screenshots took 9.5 to 10.5 s each;
    - a `snapshot -i` reported a `readMs` of 9,816.
  - On Compose's List screen, right after, three screenshots took 166 to 197 ms.
  - On Expo's Big screen, 225 to 272 ms.
- **Where the wait is not.** The agent captures on a worker of its own, apart from its tree reads, with `UiAutomation.takeScreenshot` (`Server.kt`). So the wait is in front of the capture: commands for the phone waited about 10 s behind work on Compose's 500-cell window.
- **The likely cause, not isolated.**
  - On Android 8.1 every eviction empties the whole node cache (What Android 8.1 needed in the agent). Compose's poll and every snapshot after an action evict, so each read of that window is a cold read of 591 nodes. One such read took 1.5 s in the host test.
  - Several of them, or reads they restart, fill the 10 s.
  - The agent's trace, which the daemon does not turn on, would show it.
- **The cost of the fix.** Before 0.4.3 these reads were fast and wrong: they came from a stale cache. The fallback made them right and costly on a large Compose screen.
- **A narrower eviction** would clear one window's nodes where the platform offers no per-window call. Android 8.1's cache has a private `clearNodesForWindowLocked(int)`, called under its lock; the change would be gated on the level as the rest of `Levels.kt` is.
- **The harness hid most of it.** Compose's warm snapshots, 5.9 ms at p50, came from the replica. Its first snapshots, 121.5 ms, were timed after `open --relaunch` had waited out the first tree.

### 7. The run's counts and the device's pace

- 20 measured iterations after 2 warm-up, against 50 after 3 in the emulator runs (Method). Nearest-rank p95 is then the 19th of 20 samples: one slow sample in 20 moves it, as the two unsettled cold attaches did.
- An app's relaunch takes 1.7 s (Expo) and 3.0 to 5.7 s (Compose) on this phone, against under a second on the emulator. Every flow pays one, so a flow's time here is mostly the device's.
