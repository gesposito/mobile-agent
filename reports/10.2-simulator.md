# 10.2: iOS Simulator end-to-end run

Task 10.2 of `mobile-automation-foundation` asks for three things, with no other user on the Simulator:

- The Simulator driver's gated integration tests must pass in full, with their failure messages captured. A shared-device run on 2026-09-27 failed 7 of 31 without usable messages.
- The full harness must run on iOS Simulators, for every installed runtime.
- Every Simulator gate must pass.

This report records that run, from 2026-09-28 17:20 to 2026-09-29 00:17 UTC: the setup, the two suites, the harness run, the gates, the baselines, the list-scroll numbers after task 10.17, the findings and a rerun recipe. Times are UTC. The harness's reports are committed under `reports/10.2-simulator/`, and the suites' logs under `reports/10.2-simulator/suites/`.

## Verdict

Task 10.2's checks pass. Both suites pass in full, and all seven Simulator gate checks pass on the one installed runtime.

- **Suites.**
  - The Simulator driver's suite passed 143 tests, failed none and ignored one. The ignored test is the frame pipeline's measurement. The 143 are all 105 unit tests and all 38 integration tests; 37 of the integration tests are gated on the Simulator.
  - No test failed, so there was no failure message to capture and no test to run again.
  - The daemon's device tests passed 13 of 13. The five that drive a Simulator ran on it; the eight Android tests printed why they skipped (the emulator was shut down).
  - The task counted 31 integration tests on 2026-09-27. Since then the five tests in `metadata.rs` and two input-session tests in `dtuhid.rs` have been added. So has `tree::a_build_without_the_reader_reads_through_the_translator`, which compiles only without the `reader` feature and is not in this build.
- **Runtimes.** One runtime is installed, iOS 26.5 (23F77), so "every installed runtime" is this one run on an iPhone 17 Simulator.
- **Gates.** All read at p95, all pass:
  - CLI start 5.9 ms, against 20 ms;
  - cold attach 947.0 ms (SwiftUI) and 837.9 ms (Expo), against 2 s;
  - warm snapshot of the 500-cell screen 7.9 ms (SwiftUI, against 150 ms) and 7.7 ms (Expo, against 250 ms);
  - tap dispatch 1.6 ms (SwiftUI) and 0.2 ms (Expo), against 20 ms.
  - The daemon answers the warm snapshot from its replica of the screen. A read of the SwiftUI Big screen from the device costs 150 to 178 ms ([Gates](#gates)).
- **mobile-agent's reliability.** Each of its 25 series passed all 50 measured iterations on the first attempt: CLI start once, then nine primitives and three flows on each fixture.
- **list-scroll after task 10.17** ([list-scroll](#list-scroll-after-scroll---until)).
  - SwiftUI: 16.33 s at p50 with no flake, against 25.27 s with a 16.0 % flake rate before, 35.4 % faster.
  - Expo: 15.00 s against 20.57 s, 27.1 % faster.
- **Baselines.**
  - At p50, mobile-agent was 6.05 to 8.51 times faster than Maestro 2.10.0 on all six flows.
  - It was 2.27 and 1.24 times faster than agent-device 0.21.14 on the two flows agent-device completed, SwiftUI login and big-screen. agent-device failed its golden check on the other four (finding 3).
  - Maestro failed 26 % of its Expo list-scroll iterations and flaked in 22 % (finding 4).
- **Findings.** None is in mobile-agent's code.
  1. The device tests' support strips `MOBILE_AGENT_DRIVERS` from the daemons it starts.
  2. On iOS, the bench's hand-off leaves agent-device's daemon and runner up for about 5 minutes, overlapping three of mobile-agent's series windows. They stayed idle, and no series drifted.
  3. agent-device fails four of the six flows.
  4. Maestro taps the Expo fixture's `row-200` through the tab bar.
  5. Tap to tree delta comes in two clusters, which no gate covers.

### Method

- The two suites ran first, then the harness, all from the worktree with HEAD at e84d86e and no tracked change. The report's provenance says `e84d86e11072afabef2a53bef37aa78e73beced3 (clean)`.
- The worktree has no `fixtures/*/build`. `MOBILE_AGENT_SWIFTUI_APP` and `MOBILE_AGENT_EXPO_APP` named the fixtures' built bundles for the suites.
- The test builds relink `target/release/mobile-agent` and the daemon with the test targets' features. A second `SKIP_AGENT_BUILD=1 cargo build --release` put the plain build's binaries back before the harness; nothing was compiled.
- The harness's environment:
  - `MOBILE_AGENT_DRIVERS=simulator`, so its daemons never listed Android targets;
  - `MOBILE_AGENT_SOCKET_DIR` and `AGENT_DEVICE_STATE_DIR` in private directories under `/tmp`, so neither tool's daemon was shared. A socket directory under `$TMPDIR`, or `/tmp` spelled `/private/tmp`, takes the socket path past its 103-byte limit.
- The suites ran with `MOBILE_AGENT_DRIVERS=simulator` too. The daemon tests' support removes it from the daemons they start (finding 1); with the emulator shut down, those daemons' Android driver found no device.
- Before the harness, the fixtures were terminated, no tool's process ran on the host, and only the two fixtures were installed on the Simulator besides Apple's apps.
- Load:
  - The harness started once the 1-minute load average had stayed under 4 for a minute, polled every 5 s.
  - A logger sampled the load average and the three busiest processes every 20 s through the session.
  - A second logger recorded every 20 s which tools' processes were alive: agent-device's daemon, its `xcodebuild` runner and its snapshot bridge, Maestro, and mobile-agent's daemon.
  - No other build or test ran on the host during the harness. Other processes did run, and [Load](#load) names them.

What each gate measured:

- **CLI start** is one `mobile-agent --version` process, timed around its run. No shim stood in front of it, unlike the 10.1 run's.
- **Cold attach** stops the daemon, then times the `open` that starts a daemon, attaches the Simulator and brings the fixture up.
  - The settle inside that `open` took about 220 ms.
  - This run did not split the rest. The design's components would account for most of it: CoreSimulator's loading in a new process (about 330 ms, design D6) and the reader's spawn (280 to 300 ms warm in the suite).
- **Warm snapshot** is one `snapshot -i` of the settled 500-cell screen.
  - The daemon answers it from the mirror's replica, which frames and polls keep current (design D5). No device read enters the span: 1.4 ms of the daemon suite's 9.5 ms was on the socket.

  - The gate therefore holds for a screen the replica holds. The first snapshot after a relaunch, which waits for reads, took 314.1 ms (SwiftUI) and 272.3 ms (Expo) at p50.
- **Tap dispatch** is the click reply's `dispatchMs`: from the tap's dispatch, once its element is resolved, until the target accepts the input (`crates/cli/README.md`, timing spans).
  - SwiftUI's p95, 1.6 ms, is 23 times its p50 of 0.07 ms. Expo's stays within 0.19 ms.
  - Both are far under 20 ms. S3 measured a touch-down reaching XPC in 0.05 to 0.16 ms at p95.

## list-scroll after `scroll --until`

Task 10.17 made the harness's list-scroll flow for mobile-agent a single `scroll --timeout 90000 --until id=row-200 -- down 600`, and cited this run for the Simulator's numbers after it.

What differs between the two measurements:

- **Code.** Between the before run's bench (dd4ed5a) and this run (e84d86e), only the bench's CLI adapter changed (3da516f). The CLI and daemon code are the same.
- **How they ran.** The before run measured the flow alone (`--flow list-scroll --no-baselines`). This one measured it inside the full run, between the other tools' series.

p50 / p95 / max in milliseconds over 50 measured iterations:

| Fixture | Before: the adapter's loop (`reports/10.17-list-scroll/simulator-before/`) | After: `scroll --until` (this run) | After − before, p50 / p95 |
|---|---|---|---|
| swiftui | 25265.455 / 25517.752 / 25594.456, flake 16.0 % | 16327.813 / 16805.219 / 17237.446, flake 0.0 % | −8937.642 (−35.4 %) / −8712.533 (−34.1 %) |
| expo | 20565.332 / 21033.210 / 21111.500, flake 0.0 % | 14995.416 / 15678.675 / 15778.147, flake 0.0 % | −5569.916 (−27.1 %) / −5354.535 (−25.5 %) |

Both after-series had a 0.0 % failure rate.

- **The scroll.**
  - Before, the adapter's loop took SwiftUI's list in 6 or 7 flinging strokes, each followed by a settle, and Expo's in 6.
  - After, each iteration sent one `scroll --until`, whose reply counted 17 or 18 resting strokes:

| Fixture | 17 strokes | 18 strokes |
|---|---|---|
| SwiftUI | 18 iterations, median 15.49 s | 32 iterations, median 16.61 s |
| Expo | 29 iterations, median 14.75 s | 21 iterations, median 15.37 s |

- **Why it is faster.** Design D8 measured the settle after a flinging 600-point stroke at 2.8 to 2.9 s on this Simulator, and 192 to 302 ms after a resting one on either device. The flow now makes nearly three times the strokes and spends far less time settling.
- **Where the scroll stopped.** The reply's `visible` at the stop:
  - SwiftUI: 1.0 in 38 iterations, and 0.52 to 0.98 in 12;
  - Expo: 1.0 in 35, and 0.83 to 0.99 in 15.
- **The click.** The click on `row-200` opened its detail on the first attempt in every iteration.
  - Before, SwiftUI flaked in 8 iterations. In 5, the loop had stopped with the row's centre below the screen; in 3, `Detail 200` did not appear.
  - The daemon's mostly-in-view rule (task 10.15) now decides where the scroll stops.
- **Against the scroll alone.** In the daemon suite, `scroll --until` alone took `row-200` in 12.65 to 14.87 s (SwiftUI) and 12.00 to 13.07 s (Expo). The flow adds the relaunch, the tab's tap, the row's tap and the wait for `Detail 200`.
- **Conditions.**
  - The after-series ran with the 1-minute load at 3.5 to 13.7 (median 6.0, SwiftUI) and 3.1 to 22.6 (median 7.8, Expo). The before run ran at 2.2 to 4.0, and 4.7 to 11.7 during its Expo series.
  - The first 16 measured SwiftUI iterations ran while agent-device's idle daemon and runner were still up (finding 2).
    - Their median, 16.26 s, is close to the other 34's, 16.36 s.
    - Among the first seven iterations, the 17-stroke ones (0, 2, 4, 5 and 6) took 15.9 to 16.3 s, against 15.1 to 15.8 s for every later 17-stroke one. The 18-stroke ones among them (1 and 3, 16.6 and 16.7 s) matched the later ones (15.9 to 17.2 s).
    - Iterations 7 to 15, inside the same overlap, matched the rest, so the overlap does not explain the first seven. Their cause was not found, and they do not move p50 or p95.

## Findings

Findings 1 to 3 concern the test support, the bench and agent-device. Finding 4 is Maestro's, and finding 5 is an observation that no gate covers. The evidence is here so that each can be worked from this report.

### 1. `MOBILE_AGENT_DRIVERS` does not reach the device tests' daemons

- **The cause.** `crates/daemon/tests/support/mod.rs` removes every `MOBILE_AGENT_*` variable from each daemon it starts (`clean_env`, called by `Builder::spawn`) and from each CLI run (`Env::cli` in `tests/devices.rs`). `Env::start_with` passes on only `MOBILE_AGENT_JAR`.
- **The effect.** The daemons of `tests/devices.rs` never see `MOBILE_AGENT_DRIVERS`. Started without `--drivers`, they register every driver the host runs: on macOS, the Simulator's and Android's.
  - Their discovery, which a command naming the Simulator by its UDID sets off, therefore also asks the adb server for its devices (`host:devices-l`).
  - The Android driver does no more than list: it attaches only to a target a command names.
- **In this run** the emulator was shut down. `doctor`, run once the same way, reported the adb server running with "no emulator is running", so the listing found nothing and no test touched Android.
- **The risk.** On a host where an Android device or emulator is connected, a Simulator-only run of these tests still reaches the adb server. The `devices` test would also list the Android target, which it then ignores unless `MOBILE_AGENT_ANDROID_SERIAL` is set.
- **A possible fix:** pass `MOBILE_AGENT_DRIVERS` through in `Env::start_with` as `MOBILE_AGENT_JAR` is, or give each Simulator test's daemon `--drivers simulator`.
- **The 10.19 rerun.** Task 10.19 made the support pass `MOBILE_AGENT_DRIVERS` on: `clean_env` keeps it, and only a daemon with fake targets goes without it, since device drivers named beside fake targets register too. Its test, `tests/drivers.rs`, failed first. The daemon's device tests then ran again on 2026-10-07 from 00:22:34 to 00:26:19 UTC, with the command in [Gated suites](#gated-suites) and `SKIP_AGENT_BUILD=1`, on another Simulator, `agent-device-bench` (iPhone 17 Pro, iOS 26.5), with the fixtures' bundles of [Setup](#setup) installed. The emulator was shut down; five other Simulators were booted, three of them another session's and two in use by other tasks of this one.
  - **Result.** 13 passed and none failed, in 219 s: the 5 Simulator tests ran and the 8 Android tests skipped. The 1-minute load was 4.78 at the start and 31.0 at the end, when another session's build had started.
  - **Android off.** A sampler read each daemon's environment (`ps -E`) every 0.5 s. All 5 daemons the run started, one per Simulator test, carried `MOBILE_AGENT_DRIVERS=simulator` and no `--drivers`. A daemon started so registers the Simulator driver alone (`tests/drivers.rs` reads `doctor`'s `["simulator"]`), and the Android driver is the daemon's only adb client.
  - **adb.** Before and after the run, the only adb process was the server running since 2026-09-26, and the sampler saw no other. The CLI's own `doctor`, which one of the tests runs, still asks that server for its version and runs `adb devices` when it answers, whatever the daemon's drivers.
  - **What the tests printed**, with this run's figures first and the run above's in brackets: `open` 3,363 ms (2,723); a warm snapshot 7.2 ms at p50 (9.5); the click's dispatch 0.107 ms (0.130), its first frame change at 71.6 ms (67.1) and first tree delta at 123.7 ms (124.5); `scroll --until` to `row-200` in 12.9 to 13.6 s on SwiftUI (12.65 to 14.87) and 12.4 to 13.1 s on Expo (12.00 to 13.07).

### 2. On iOS, the bench's hand-off leaves agent-device's daemon and runner up for about 5 minutes

- **The mechanism.**
  - agent-device's release in the bench is `agent-device close --json --platform ios --udid <udid> --session mobile-agent-bench`, which closes the session.
  - On the Simulator, agent-device's daemon keeps its XCTest runner (`xcodebuild test-without-building ... AgentDeviceRunnerUITests/RunnerTests/testCommand`) and its snapshot bridge (`snapshot-bridge serve`, spawned in the Simulator) until its own idle exit, about 5 minutes after its last request.
  - At 20:59:44.734 its log recorded `ios_runner_session_detach_skipped ... reason runner_never_served_a_command` as it stopped. That was 5 minutes after the close that followed its last batch, which began at 20:54:41.
- **The overlap.** The process log shows the three running into three mobile-agent windows:

| Window (UTC) | agent-device's last request | mobile-agent series that overlapped |
|---|---|---|
| 17:54:07 to about 17:59:20 | its golden checks | CLI start; SwiftUI cold attach, first and warm snapshot, tap dispatch |
| 18:37:36 to about 18:42:50 | the SwiftUI login series | the first 16 measured iterations of SwiftUI list-scroll |
| 20:54:41 to 20:59:44 | the SwiftUI big-screen series | Expo cold attach, first and warm snapshot, tap dispatch |

- **The effect.** They were idle: none appeared among the three busiest processes in any sample of those windows, and none of the series drifted.
  - Cold attach: 923.2 ms at the median in the first half, 922.2 in the second.
  - Warm snapshot: 6.4 and 6.0 ms.
  - list-scroll: see its conditions above.
  - The series inside those windows have no measurement without the overlap to compare with. The gates they feed pass by wide margins.
- **Unlike Android** (10.1's finding 4), the runner holds no UI automation role the next tool needs.
- **A possible fix:** stop agent-device's daemon at the release, or wait for it to exit, so that each tool measures alone.
- **Maestro** leaves nothing running: its `xcodebuild` and XCTest runner ended with each `maestro test`.
- **The 10.18 rerun.** On a Simulator, task 10.18 made agent-device's release run `agent-device daemon stop --clean --json` after `close`. A bare `daemon stop` leaves the runner running for the next daemon to adopt; `--clean` kills it. The hand-off then waits, for at most 30 s, until none of agent-device's daemon, runner and bridge is left (`crates/bench/README.md`, Hand-offs between tools). Its tests in `baselines` and `handoff::tests` failed first. A login series then ran on 2026-10-07 from 01:33:54 to 01:35:18 UTC on another Simulator, `agent-device-bench` (iPhone 17 Pro, iOS 26.5). It ran the SwiftUI fixture with `--flow login --iterations 3 --warmup 1 --retries 0`, agent-device 0.21.14 and no Maestro on `PATH`. The 1-minute load was 10.1 at the start and 23.2 at the end, from other sessions' builds. Both tools passed every iteration.
  - **The release.** Before `mobile-agent`'s timed series, agent-device's hand-off took 818.7 ms: `close`, `daemon stop --clean`, `session state-dir` and the process listings. The one at the end of the run took 649.5 ms. Neither recorded an error. In the run above, `close` alone took 144 to 190 ms.
  - **The overlap.** A sampler listed the processes every 0.5 s. agent-device's daemon, `xcodebuild`, runner app, bridge `simctl spawn` and bridge were all up at 01:34:23.504 and all gone at 01:34:24.003, while `daemon stop` was still running. `mobile-agent`'s daemon started at 01:34:24.508 for its first `open`. None of the five appeared in the 31 samples of `mobile-agent`'s series, from 01:34:24.5 to 01:34:40.0, where the run above had them up for about 5 minutes.

### 3. agent-device 0.21.14 did not time four of the six flows

Every failure below happened in the golden checks, which each tool gets two tries at, so these flows were not timed:

- **SwiftUI list-scroll**, both tries: `Batch failed at step 3 (scroll): scroll down spent its 12-pass budget without id="row-200" becoming visible`, after 11.2 to 11.4 s of scrolling. Its budget is fixed, as on Android in the 10.1 run.
- **Every Expo flow**, both tries each. The step after `open` failed at once:
  - login: `Batch failed at step 2 (fill): Selector did not match: id="login-username"`;
  - big-screen: `Batch failed at step 2 (press): Selector did not match: id="tab-big"`;
  - list-scroll's second try: `... (press): Selector did not match: id="tab-list"`.
  - In agent-device's session events, those steps failed 210 to 250 ms after `open` returned.
  - In the one Expo try that got past the step, list-scroll's first, the press on `tab-list` landed 870 ms after `open`. Its scroll then ran out of the 12 passes.
- **The likely cause** (not verified): right after a launch, the Expo fixture's window holds only its launch splash, an empty React Native root, for a few hundred ms (task 8.15), and agent-device's step does not wait past it. This run did not read the screen agent-device saw.
- **Where agent-device did run**, on SwiftUI login and big-screen, it passed every measured iteration.
  - One warm-up iteration of big-screen failed its first try with `Selector did not match: id="tab-big"` and passed on the retry.
  - It reported its own duration beside each sample: 8.39 to 8.73 s for login, and 2.80 to 3.41 s for big-screen.

### 4. Maestro 2.10.0 taps the Expo fixture's `row-200` through the tab bar

- **What failed.** In the Expo list-scroll series, 13 of 50 iterations failed and 11 were flaky.
  - All 37 failed attempts stopped at the flow's last step: `Assert that "Detail 200", id: detail-200 is visible FAILED`.
  - Each came after a completed `scrollUntilVisible` and a completed `Tap on id: row-200`.
- **The failed attempt at 22:01 UTC**, from Maestro's own log (debug directory `2026-09-29_000100`, local time):
  - The scroll stopped with `row-200`'s bounds at `[0,792][402,840]`, below the top of the tab bar, which Maestro read at `[80,784][160,840]` for `tab-list`.
  - The tap went to the row's centre, `(201, 816)`, which is on the tab bar's middle button.
  - The screenshot at the failed assertion shows the Big tab selected.
- **SwiftUI.** Maestro's same flow passed 50 of 50: the SwiftUI list keeps its rows clear of the tab bar with its content insets. mobile-agent's `scroll --until` passed 50 of 50 on both fixtures.
- **What the bench recorded.** The bench reports Maestro's last output lines, so the committed errors read `This could be a real regression ...` and the debug directory, not the failed assertion. The same was true of the timeout in 10.1's finding 6.

### 5. Tap to tree delta comes in two clusters

- **The numbers.** The span from a click's dispatch to its first tree change clusters on both fixtures:
  - SwiftUI: 79 to 82 ms in 11 of 50 taps, and 328 to 350 ms in 39;
  - Expo: 22 to 30 ms in 19, and 248 to 283 ms in 31.
  - Within each cluster the spread is a few milliseconds.
- **A reading, not verified:** the first re-read after the tap sometimes precedes the application's change to its tree, and the next one comes only once the frames stop.
  - The Simulator sends no change events, and the mirror re-reads after input and after frames stop changing (design D5).
  - A settle on SwiftUI's detail screen took 436 ms at p50, ended by the frames.
- **Scope.** No gate covers this span on the Simulator. It bounds how soon an agent's next snapshot shows a tap's effect.

## Rerun

Build without the Android agent, then run the suites with the fixtures' bundles named (from a checkout whose `fixtures/*/build` holds them, the two variables are not needed):

```sh
export SKIP_AGENT_BUILD=1
cargo build --release                             # cli, daemon and bench
export MOBILE_AGENT_SWIFTUI_APP=<FixtureSwiftUI.app> MOBILE_AGENT_EXPO_APP=<FixtureExpo.app>
MOBILE_AGENT_SIM_UDID=<udid> \
  cargo test --release -p simulator --tests --no-fail-fast -- --nocapture --test-threads=1
MOBILE_AGENT_SIM_UDID=<udid> MOBILE_AGENT_DRIVERS=simulator \
  cargo test --release -p daemon --test devices --no-fail-fast -- --nocapture --test-threads=1
cargo build --release                             # the plain binaries again, which the test builds relinked
```

Before the harness:

- terminate the fixtures (`xcrun simctl terminate <udid> dev.mobileagent.fixture.swiftui`, and `.expo`);
- wait until the 1-minute load has stayed under 4 for a minute;
- give both tools private state under a short path.

```sh
export MOBILE_AGENT_DRIVERS=simulator MOBILE_AGENT_SOCKET_DIR=/tmp/<short private directory> \
  AGENT_DEVICE_STATE_DIR=/tmp/<another>
target/release/mobile-agent-bench run --target <udid> --cli target/release/mobile-agent \
  --out reports/10.2-simulator
```

- **Duration.** The run takes about 6.5 hours. Maestro's two list-scroll series take 3.5 of them, at about 100 s per SwiftUI run and 90 s per Expo run, the Expo series with 24 retries.
- **agent-device on PATH.** agent-device is installed under mise's Node, so the harness needs `eval "$(mise env)"` on PATH to find it.

The committed files:

- `reports/10.2-simulator/report.json` and `report.md`, as the bench wrote them, with one edit:
  - The home directory in Maestro's debug directories, in 35 of the JSON's error messages, is written as `$HOME`. The markdown has none.
  - The edit went through the bench's own reader and writer (`Report::from_json`, then `Report::write`). They reproduce the unedited pair byte for byte, so the JSON and markdown still agree.
  - The markdown came out unchanged, and the JSON differs from the original only in those paths.
- `reports/10.2-simulator/flows/`, the baseline flow files as the bench last wrote them:
  - Maestro's for Expo;
  - agent-device's login and big-screen for SwiftUI, and its list-scroll for Expo.
  - A flow's file differs between the fixtures only in the app id.
- `reports/10.2-simulator/suites/`, the two suites' output, with the checkout written as `<repo>` and the home directory as `$HOME`.
- The CLI adapter's screenshots (`cli/`) are not committed.

Left behind:

- The Simulator was left booted with both fixtures terminated.
- agent-device's runner app (`com.callstack.agentdevice.runner.uitests.xctrunner`) is still installed on it. Maestro's driver was not installed at the end.
- No daemon, reader or tool process of the run remained on the host.
